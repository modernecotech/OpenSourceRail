## Independent stop from 72 km/h

This sensitivity uses a 2% descent, two seconds of coast and a 0.60 m/s² net stop. The fault case has no wheel braking, no regeneration and one unavailable rail module. Eleven active modules share the demand.

| Six-car steel load | Force per active module | Rail and shoe energy per active module | Peak rail interface power per active module | Stop at 14.6 kN per active module |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Central | 14.38 kN | 1.384 kWh | 293 kW | 379.9 m |
| High | 17.81 kN | 1.714 kWh | 363 kW | 495.7 m |

The stated net target needs 386.9 m including the two-second coast. The older **14.6 kN** figure is a rounded required force at a different crowd-mass basis. Holding that number constant increases the high-load stop by 108.8 m. At 1.0 m/s² net, the distance falls to 248.3 m, and one-out independent module demands rise to 21.60 and 26.75 kN. The unchanged service force corresponding to 0.60 m/s² on level track gives a longer downgrade stop. Wheel-braked cases constrain force by static load and adhesion and include loss of the heaviest axle. Authenticated force curves, response time, failed-channel behaviour, thermal fade, rail damage and battery excitation energy remain open. The constant-force calculation reports frictional heat shared by shoe and rail. The heat split, repetition limit and cooling are equipment properties. Supply a surveyed **front-to-protected-limit** distance and withheld overlap together to calculate stopping margin. The report also gives stop distance plus train length and overlap as an illustrative spatial separation. Establish block occupancy, turnout and tail-release limits with signalling and the actual alignment. Interstation distance and diagrammed blocks provide no protection limit.

The station handover bench records door, parking, hold and traction signals; it has no approach stopping limit or overlap input. The generic ATP arithmetic tests use a two-car, 40 t synthetic consist with 400 ms reaction and a 1.2–1.5 m/s² emergency curve. Those numbers are not an Airport brake setting. A matched ATP comparison needs an Airport consist record bound to this vehicle's physical and duty IDs, a measured degraded brake curve and response time, and surveyed direction-specific front-to-limit distance with withheld overlap. The high-load 495.7 m comparator above is a planning force sensitivity, not a guaranteed stop; the 386.9 m target requires the unproved per-module force and cooling duty.

## Traction, support and boarding

The Airport candidate declares four driven axles per car. The traction calculation compares driving the forward pair, the rear pair and both bogies, then loses the heaviest driven axle in each arrangement. The partial-drive rows are analytical necessary-bound comparisons, not candidate configurations: they have no corresponding installed mass, bogie, brake, protection or cost assembly. The force margin retains the system's 0.10 adhesion, resistance and reserve. On a 2% upgrade the combined duty requires 95.71% of static axle load on powered axles. Losing the heaviest driven axle leaves 95.62% centrally and 95.44% in the high case with both bogies powered. This consumes the declared reserve in both cases. Request hot tractive effort versus speed, driven axle assignments and the converter fault split. [Local traction inputs](../../system.toml)

The independent 65 kWh service battery has 52.0 kWh usable when new against a 50.4 kWh request. At 80% retained capacity, usable energy falls to 41.6 kWh, an 8.8 kWh shortfall before hot derating. Meeting the request at that retained capacity would require a hot capacity factor of 1.2115 relative to the unheated calculation, so no further capacity derating at or below 1 can close the gap. The audit shows hypothetical factors of 1.0, 0.9 and 0.8, corresponding to minimum nominal capacities of 78.75, 87.50 and 98.44 kWh. These are energy-only sensitivities, not hot pack ratings. The actual hot usable energy, voltage and discharge power for braking and egress remain unmeasured.

### Preliminary correction and procurement envelopes

The unchanged high-load, 2% upgrade and 0.60 m/s² net acceleration duty needs 200.80 kN at the wheels after loss of the heaviest driven axle. The 1.15 adhesion reserve makes the static adhesion force requirement 230.92 kN. At the assumed 0.10 rail adhesion and unchanged total mass, the lost axle must carry no more than **10,565 kg**, versus **11,214 kg** in the present high case. Both front-bogie axles have that heaviest load, so a constant-mass revision must move at least **648 kg off each** (1,297 kg per car) while keeping every receiving axle below the allowable maximum. The central case requires 178 kg off each heaviest axle. This is a necessary static load bound; a real layout must show the installed CG, all 24 reactions, motor and converter fault split, adhesion, hot force curve, cooling and recovery. If load distribution stays unchanged, the high case requires at least 0.100276 adhesion including the declared reserve, above the 0.10 assumption. The audit does not replace the required acceleration with a lower value. At constant full effort through the 72 km/h cap, the high case requires 4.016 MW wheel and 4.898 MW electrical input at the stated 0.82 efficiency. This is an equipment quote envelope, not a sustained-speed operating point. Any redistribution or added battery mass changes the axle and braking cases and must be recomputed. [Traction assumptions](../../system.toml)

The battery quote envelope separates **36 kWh** of two-hour essential loads, **4 kWh** brake event allocation and **2 kWh** accessible egress allocation, then applies the existing 20% reserve to require **50.4 kWh delivered hot at end of life**. It requires at least 18 kW continuous hot discharge. Event kWh does not establish event peak kW, voltage sag, duration or simultaneous discharge. Request those measured duty traces and pack ratings, plus charging acceptance, cooling, enclosure, BMS, isolation, mounts, cabling, fire protection, fitted dimensions, local repair route and replacement cost. The existing 65 kWh pack has a **500 kg** mass allowance and **$100,000** vehicle-price allocation, not a measured specific energy or scalable supplier quote. A larger pack's full installed mass and price replace those allowances; the change enters all train masses and moments, traction, braking, civil and cash. Full-nominal two-hour recharge input would be 43.75, 48.61 or 54.69 kW for the three energy-only nominal thresholds at 90% charging efficiency. Actual timetable charging depends on the energy removed and pack acceptance. [Service-power assumptions](../../system.toml)

`battery-trial.json` is an identified **planning estimate** for regression, not supplier evidence or a selected design. It specifies 100 kWh, 0.8 new usable fraction, 0.8 retention and 0.8 hypothetical hot factor: 51.2 kWh deliverable and 0.8 kWh energy margin. Its 800 kg fitted mass is a rounded assumption above proportional scaling of the current 500 kg allowance (500 × 100/65 = 769 kg); its $160,000 price is a rounded assumption above proportional scaling of the current $100,000 allocation ($153,846). The 0.0 m longitudinal and 0.85 m height centroids are packaging assumptions. The resulting 300 kg added train mass and $60,000 price delta are study inputs only. The 30 kW continuous, 100 kW for 30 s peak ratings are assumptions pending the brake and egress waveform. The full-nominal two-hour recharge input is 55.56 kW. This pack passes the energy necessary screen only; peak power, protection, heat, physical fit, fire and life remain open. The trial's digest binds this guide as its source. A supplier quote instead requires a local `source_document` with a matching SHA-256 and will still need authentication and duty tests.

### Owner-supplied saloon capacity study

The existing 700-place Airport train is retained as a historical planning reference, not a proven accessible fit. [The Airport vehicle statement](../../VEHICLE.md) calls for **20 seats and two wheelchair places per car**, four 1.3 m clear door openings on each side, a 17 m body and 2.7 m outside width. `saloon-trial.json` declares a smaller **16.0 × 2.4 m** interior rectangle as an illustrative packaging assumption. It allocates 20 seats at a declared 0.45 m²/place, two wheelchair spaces at a declared 1.5 m²/place, a 0.9 m wide clear longitudinal aisle between door zones, four 1.3 m wide door-access bands spanning the interior, and 1.575 m² of explicit standing zones at a declared three persons/m². Each seat has a 0.50 × 0.70 m axis-aligned trial footprint; each wheelchair zone has a 2.00 × 0.75 m trial footprint. Rotation is not assumed. Whole-person footprint packing and standing rounding yield **four standees and 26 normal places/car**, or **156 places/six-car train**. Unallocated floor does not create places. The seat and wheelchair counts match the original planning counts, so no seat-count price saving is claimed; installed interior mass and price remain unknown.

The layout rectangle checks compare each zone with the stated interior and every door band with [system geometry](../../system.toml). They reject overlap, count whole declared furniture footprints inside their own zones, and require each wheelchair zone and clear aisle segment to connect by shared edges to a door-access zone. The declared furniture footprints, seat area, wheelchair area, aisle width and standing density are *study assumptions*, not approved accessibility, crowd, fire or egress limits. Drawings must establish real seat pitch, structural intrusions, wall thickness, wheelchair maneuvering, continuous accessible path, dual-side boarding, restraint and evacuation before capacity or fit can be accepted. The same layout's high-load study retains the **Airport's 700-per-six crowd comparator** and its adverse +1.8 m passenger centroid where that governs mass and axle reactions. The separate 160-person/car structural control for the v0.6 reference is not transferred silently to this Airport candidate. Actual service loading remains at most 26/car; the 700-person high case is a physical strength and braking control, not an offered passenger count.

The 156 mm nominal secondary-seat gap leaves +11, +1 and −9 mm before shims and tolerances for the three trial stacks. The 0.6 kN/mm spring consumes 40.2 mm of a provisional 40 mm payload stroke in the central case and 55.0 mm in the high case. The stock door allocation has 10 mm margin and 2 mm when loaded distortion doubles. The 75 mm static horizontal platform gap needs a boarding survey across actual wear, suspension and stopping poses. The bogie paths retain the bare-stock swept boxes and rigid transforms for both ends. Verify the equipped paths with tooling, lifting, restraint, cables and hoses. The alternative body materials inherit none of the steel fitted results. ## Reproduce or attach a return

```sh
python3 -m cities.Baghdad.engineering.simulation.vehicle_audit > build/vehicle-audit.json
python3 -m cities.Baghdad.engineering.simulation.vehicle_audit --battery-alternative cities/Baghdad/engineering/simulation/battery-trial.json > build/vehicle-battery-trial.json
python3 -m cities.Baghdad.engineering.simulation.vehicle_audit --battery-alternative cities/Baghdad/engineering/simulation/battery-trial.json --saloon-layout cities/Baghdad/engineering/simulation/saloon-trial.json > build/vehicle-combined-trial.json
python3 -m unittest cities.Baghdad.engineering.simulation.test_vehicle_audit
```

The output includes a physical candidate ID, duty ID, evaluation ID and source digests. A new load, grade, block assumption or source revision requires its own evaluation. Use the `return_template` as a separate local JSON file. Set `kind` to `supplier-declaration`, `physical-measurement` or `synthetic-fixture`. Under `documents`, record original local filenames with issuer, date and SHA-256. Under `returns`, name the evidence scope and cite the document, article serial, procedure revision and witness. Give the file to `--evidence-return` on the standalone audit.

The optional `--battery-alternative` JSON is a complete study configuration, not a return on the original 65 kWh candidate. It requires `basis_kind` (`planning-estimate` or `supplier-quote`), `supplier_reference`, `quote_source_sha256`, nominal energy, usable/retained/hot fractions, full installed mass and price, car-centred x/z location, hot continuous and peak kW and peak duration. A `supplier-quote` also requires `source_document`, checked relative to the JSON directory. Planning estimates should provide the source document too. Installed technical characteristics enter a new physical candidate ID; source and commercial values affect its evaluation only. The report gives `battery.alternative.price_delta_usd` and `mass_delta_kg` as **replacements** for the existing per-train allocation. It does not integrate a changed price into a city cash total or qualify the revised pack physically.

`--saloon-layout` takes a separate, source-bound `airport-saloon-layout/1` JSON tied to the parent Airport article. It reports normal seats, wheelchair spaces and whole standing places per car; the resulting normal cap propagates to the service offer and boarding calculation. Its high mass case retains the Airport high/crowd comparator independently of the normal cap. Layout drawings, supplier fitout masses and prices, evacuation and accessibility approval remain open.

For the integrated Airport audit, generate the form from `vehicle.return_template` in the JSON for the formation, material and grade under review. Reuse the same arguments when attaching the return.
