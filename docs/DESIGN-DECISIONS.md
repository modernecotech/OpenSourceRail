# Choosing a railway for a corridor

Start with the busiest hour in each direction, the journey time and waiting time passengers need, the accessible boarding arrangement and the streets available for construction. Compare trains, power and civil works against that same brief. A comparison should identify the cheapest offer that meets those requirements, or tell the buyer which missing price or test could change the decision.

**What went wrong in the earlier comparisons.** The network estimator fixed a span and pier arrangement, picked the lowest priced supply case, then ran the civil comparisons using that supply. Its published summary called the associated equipment “selected”. The separate 15-case integrated catalogue assessed every longer span with a six-car steel train and radial supply, and every three-car material option with a 10 m span and paired supply. Zero of its fifteen cases cleared all decision holds. These calculations answered narrower questions than their labels suggested. **Keep three kinds of decision visible.** Safety, accessible use and passenger protection govern every option. Local manufacture and repair, low lifetime cost, elevated construction and catenary-free supply guide the design. Formation, capacity policy, speed, equipment, station arrangement and construction method need corridor-specific choices. The development vehicle has six 17 m wheel-supported cars, 44 fixed seats and two wheelchair places per car, one boarding side and an independently sized service battery. Its passive aluminium propulsion interface leaves the installed drive open. The synthetic corridor provides a repeatable software comparison. The city opening provides drawings and supplier schedules. Both examples remain accessible through their own guides. ## Decisions a buyer should reopen

| Decision | What the evidence changes | What decides the corridor choice |
|---|---|---|
| Alignment, station spacing and first opening | Five mapped stop centroids span at least 15.09 km in route order in a 12 km operating example. Central hourly demand there implies 103 people on the busiest 15-minute departure leg. Both geometry and ridership could change materially. | Survey station entrances, utilities, street crossings and grades. Compare urban and airport markets on observed hourly origin–destination counts, accessible transfer time, paid passenger-km and public cost. [Corridor and demand assumptions](../cities/Baghdad/engineering/simulation/PLAN.md#demand-walking-and-routing-handoff) |
| Formation, seating and platforms | The six-car development vehicle offers 276 normal places. The separate powered-wheel city proposal assumes 700, or 350 in three cars, with a different door and occupancy policy. A shorter train can retain the longer platform for expansion or use a shorter first build with priced extensions. | Fix peak onboard demand, crowding policy, wheelchair provision, worst lift outage and required future throughput. Size cars, doors, access and platform together. Keep crowd and accidental loads in the structural checks. [Vehicle assumptions](../cities/Baghdad/system.toml) · [Rail capacity and station dwell methods](https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/tcrp/tcrp_rpt_165ch-08.pdf) |
| Track, curves and station placement | A 1.435 m gauge fixes the development reference’s wheel/rail interface. Bringing the same straight 75 mm platform offset around a 150 m illustrative curve produces 62 mm of inside-body interference. | Cost straight station approaches, a different site or a revised vehicle/edge geometry. Include turnout leads, swept clearance, accessible boarding, rescue access and available rail, wheelset and maintenance tooling in the same bid. [Track reference](../engineering/station_rail/brief.toml) · [Curve and doorway sweep](../cities/Baghdad/engineering/prototype/infrastructure/PLATFORM-SWEEP.md) |
| Speed, frequency, fleet and reserve | 15/20/30-minute headways give average scheduled waits of 7.5/10/15 minutes for random arrivals. On the six-car 30-site case the diagram needs 3/3/2 active sets. Adding the same three spares gives 6/6/5 purchased. At 20 minutes a changed return phase clears a terminal conflict. | Set a maximum acceptable wait and section journey time before pricing frequencies. Derive reserve from availability, inspection time and rescue coverage. Check turnbacks, block release, missed trips, gradients and train-specific stopping distance at each speed. [Timetable and native crosschecks](../cities/Baghdad/engineering/simulation/MOTION-POWER.md) |
| Traction and power supply | Station-powered, onboard wheel-traction, battery and mixed options change the vehicle mass, wayside equipment and rescue method. In the priced 15-minute wheel-traction case, six paired sites cost $316.46m less in opening capital than 30 radial sites. Both complete 144 normal trips. The modeled combined outage stops through service on the radial layout. | Compare the same peak demand, voltage floor, outage service, regeneration, thermal duty, fault clearance, safe isolation and evacuation. Price grid connections, protection, whole-life renewals and rescue. Paired-site renewals remain open. [Supply comparison](../cities/Baghdad/engineering/simulation/SIMULATION.md#a-journey-through-the-model) |
| Carbody, bogies and brakes | At the same three-car, 20-minute offer, steel, aluminium and hybrid loaded masses are 99.57, 96.63 and 95.96 t. The hybrid draws about 33.1 MWh less source energy per year than steel for a $4.05m opening premium. On a 2% downgrade with adhesion of 0.08, all-axle adhesion-limited deceleration is about 0.59 m/s² before other forces. A 1 m/s² braking requirement needs another demonstrated means of meeting it. | Compare fatigue, joints, repair time, wheel/rail forces, wheel-slide protection and brake heating with the actual load distribution. Under constant duty and a 4% real discount rate over 30 years, energy alone would repay the modeled hybrid premium at about $7.06/kWh. Maintenance and structural savings can change that threshold. | Doors, controls, accessibility, climate and emergency power | The city article's 1,310 mm trial opening leaves 10 mm over its 1,300 mm target, falling to 2 mm when loaded distortion doubles. The concentrated assisted transfer trial takes 52.4 s against 35 s scheduled. The 65 kWh service pack falls 8.8 kWh short at 80% remaining capacity. | Match platform side, number of openings, seals, suspension travel and stopping accuracy to the actual platform. Compare one-exit and lift-out movements through to a protected place. Size peak cooling and emergency loads separately. | Viaduct, foundations and construction | A 10 m separate-track box is one prototype. The shared engineering reference already studies a 20 m box and three guideway families. Reusing a short-span cage or crane tests that detail's range. An economical longer span needs its own tendon layout, lifting plan and supports. Delhi's 21.6–29.1 m spans used short precast segments and a two-track box, with pier sites constrained by the road below. | Compare a redesigned 20 m girder, a 25 m option, shorter constrained-site units and segmental erection. Compare buy, share and hire across the opening and follow-on programme. Carry verified quantities, upkeep, renewals and opening delays into phased buyer cash. Treat overlapping tooling as a credit and verified local content as a separately evidenced figure. ## Does fixing one choice change the apparent winner?

An independent replay of the original **148.2 km first programme** tested all four supply options against each of its four priced spans and both pier arrangements. It held seven lines, 64 station allowances, 24.67 million annual train-km and 242 trainsets fixed. Protected conductor has the lowest modelled 30-year present cost across all eight civil layouts. Its lead over overhead is only **$51.62m–$52.54m** on a railway with about $12bn of priced lifecycle costs. Overhead serves as a conventional comparator. A change in the price or lifetime of a line-wide asset could reverse the order. All 32 cases retain open physical acceptance.

| Span and piers | Protected conductor | Overhead | Partial conductor and battery | Station charging and battery |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 m separate | 12,021 | 12,073 | 12,080 | 12,703 |
| 10 m shared | 12,099 | 12,152 | 12,159 | 12,783 |
| 20 m separate | 11,864 | 11,916 | 11,921 | 12,543 |
| 20 m shared | 11,903 | 11,955 | 11,960 | 12,583 |
| 25 m separate | 11,833 | 11,884 | 11,889 | 12,511 |
| 25 m shared | 11,864 | 11,916 | 11,921 | 12,543 |
| 30 m separate | 11,812 | 11,864 | 11,868 | 12,490 |
| 30 m shared | 11,838 | 11,890 | 11,895 | 12,517 |

Values are USD millions, rounded, on the original common planning basis. At 20 m the priced shared crosshead increases the protected-conductor total by $39.22m over separate piers. Site-specific avoided utility moves and foundations would have to cover that difference after pricing the wider head and revised footings. Longer-span sizing, crane duty and local site works can also change the order. The 12 km moving-load simulation has a different service, supply topology and pricing scope.

## When to select

Keep a design in the engineering comparison while it has an unresolved mandatory function. Publish its known cost and failed cases so a designer can see the value of correcting it. For each surviving combination, collect the same delivered scope from suppliers, compare installed and discounted lifecycle cost under the same passenger offer, and repeat the affected checks when an input changes. A cheap indicative total, a familiar precedent or a passed software test gives a reason to investigate. Selection requires an identified configuration and evidence for its governing limits. Fabrication and passenger operation have separate acceptance decisions.
