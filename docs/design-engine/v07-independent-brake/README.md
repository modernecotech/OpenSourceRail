This evaluates the [powered prototype research bundle](../v07-prototype-bundle/README.md), not the selected passive-wheel reference. It does not assign braking credit to the service battery, traction motors, wheel discs, or an attractive-force catalogue number.

The bundle's **analytical upper normal mass** is 25,071.998 kg/car, or 150,431.989 kg for six cars; installed mass and axle reactions remain unknown. Its 20 m/s, 2% downgrade, net 0.6 m/s² independent stop with all wheel brakes unavailable requires **119,773.95 N** longitudinal rail force. With one of 12 bogie modules unavailable, 11 active modules must each provide **10,888.54 N**. At 20 m/s that is **217.77 kW per active rail-contact interface initially**. Ideal constant-deceleration time and distance are 33.33 s and 333.33 m; each working module dissipates about **3.630 MJ** at the shoe/rail interface. This ideal distance excludes brake command, build-up, reaction, tolerance, hot fade, contamination, and safety margins. The rail and shoe divide the heat; it is not all shoe heat. A static 2% grade at this mass calls for **29.51 kN** train holding force before margin, or **2.683 kN per module** if 11 remain active. The larger 160-person crowd-control mass requires a **separate** brake and parking check.

Option A is a practical **test candidate**, not a closed braking architecture. Its existing mechanical allocation is 200–315 kg per two-shoe bogie module (2.4–3.78 t/train), whereas the catalogue gives 150 kg of bare shoes/module. The remaining 50–165 kg/module has no supplier-backed breakdown and cannot be treated as installed mass. A [Knorr/Zelisko MMBC control unit](https://rail.knorr-bremse.com/media/2000-products/product-broschures/e-ze-br-0004-mmtbcs-2022-v12.pdf) can monitor magnet current and rail touchdown and lists a maximum 10 kg controller mass; it does **not** supply the missing magnet coil power or certify this train's brake energy store. The controller count, enclosure, wiring and protected power architecture must be chosen and weighed.

## Physical assembly and service boundary

Use the existing car frame **x forward, y left, z up from rail head**. The two bogie frame origins are translated to car x = −6.4 and +6.4 m, with proposed shoe centre lines at bogie y = −0.7175 and +0.7175 m. Each nominal shoe is 1.0 m in x; the 1.435 m gauge and 1.7 m pair-envelope are *interface reservations*. The stowed pair box bottom is nominal z = +0.12 m and deployed contact is z = 0. These are translations in the existing candidate, with identity rotation at nominal straight track; actual bogie yaw, roll, heave, spring deflection, wheel wear and track curvature require finite rigid-transform sweeps. Static final positions cannot establish clearance to rail fasteners, crossings, points, motor/gear boxes, retained discs, cables or the car floor. The applicable [ERA track-brake interface guidance](https://www.era.europa.eu/system/files/2022-11/Guide%20for%20the%20application%20of%20the%20LOC%20%26%20PAS%20TSI%20%28EN%20Version%202.0%20-%2001_01_2015%29.pdf.pdf) requires track, switches and crossings to be compatible with magnetic braking; local railway approval and actual route geometry still govern this prototype.

The current *minimum quotation count per six-car train* is 12 bogie modules / 24 shoes; 24 lifting actuators; 24 guide slides; 48 end stops; 48 retention pins; 96 shoe-mount bolts. These counts are inherited from the [mechanical child](../v07-powered-vehicle/plan.json), **not an issued manufacturing BOM**. Add vendor bearings/bushes and wear liners, mounting bolts and lock features, cable supports, per-bogie flexible power and earth loops, protected disconnects, guards, control boxes, energy store and temperature sensing as supplier-defined line items. Their quantity, weight and price remain unknown. Require a supported downward shoe removal over a pit, isolated and discharged electrical supply, a removable shoe/liner, correct tool sweeps at full travel, replacement torque/retention records and a repeat brake test. Supplier drawings must show the guide-bracket force path into the bogie, fastener fatigue and local rail load. Do not count merely lowered shoe geometry as an installed or serviceable assembly.

## Quote and qualification request — exact missing evidence

1. **Stop and hot duty:** On the specified rail steel, head profile and wear range, submit measured longitudinal force versus speed 0–20 m/s and shoe temperature for dry, wet, oily and debris conditions; test a 20 m/s stop at ≥10.89 kN longitudinal force per active module with one of 12 modules isolated. Report command-to-contact and force build-up, stopping distance including delay, repeated-stop temperature, rail/shoe heat split, wear and damage, replacement interval, and turnouts/crossings compatibility. Test 160-person crowd mass separately.
2. **Power-loss duty:** State coil current, voltage, resistance, peak/continuous power, permissible duty and failure modes at worst temperature; actuator/drop and stow power and time; control and contact-monitoring power; complete power-source independence from OLE and traction DC; battery/energy-store chemistry, capacity, state-of-health, isolation, charge/recharge, degraded operation and proof tests. Size protected energy from the measured **time-integral** of those loads over delayed and hot one-module-out stops with margin. The existing 82.8 kWh/car service battery has no certified brake-energy credit.
3. **Parking and failures:** Provide a separately rated **power-free** 2% grade hold with the worst allowed single failure and credible contamination, or explicitly pair A with a qualified spring-applied wheel-disc parking brake and demonstrate the required *static wheel/rail adhesion*. The latter is maintainable and available from [Knorr-Bremse's spring-parking caliper range](https://rail.knorr-bremse.com/en/se/portfolio/products-and-systems/braking-systems/actuation/), but it is **not adhesion-independent** and cannot close an adhesion-independent parking requirement by declaration. Verify accidental drop, stuck-on drag, one-side-only actuation, cable break, controller loss, manual release/tow, low-speed hold, status indication and isolation rules.
4. **Installed module:** Supply controlled 3D geometry, all rigid transforms and operating swept envelopes; shoe, slide, stop, pin, actuator, guide, bracket, bearing, fastener, guard, sensor, harness and power-source masses with CG and axle reactions; lifting points, removal/tool access, fatigue and corrosion data, local fabrication drawings, QA limits, spares and repair times. Quote total installed train price and rail maintenance cost, rather than a shoe price.

The [current EN 16207:2024 publisher description](https://www.dinmedia.de/en/standard/din-en-16207/377664290) identifies magnetic-brake interface and type/vehicle testing but explicitly limits that standard to **mainline high-suspension** systems. The proposed urban arrangement has no approved suspension classification; it must obtain an applicable urban brake requirements basis and independent safety review. EN 16207 cannot be used as automatic acceptance. ERA guidance supports emergency magnetic brakes only with compatible infrastructure and addresses brake-state indication. Neither guidance nor a catalogue rating substitutes for supplier tests on the selected rail.

**Decision:** commission competing electromagnetic-rail-brake quotations and one selected-rail instrumented test; retain a separate parking-brake work item. The necessary dynamic ratio of 0.0756 makes the arrangement worth measuring, but there is **no source-backed longitudinal force, protected electrical duty, power-free rail parking design, complete mass or rail-compatibility evidence** to close it today. The prototype bundle's `physical_accepted`, `manufacturing_release` and `operating_release` remain false. An approved stop envelope, workshop drawing and safety case must follow supplier data and independent review.
