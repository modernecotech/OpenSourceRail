# Counted support and guidance architecture continuation

The speed or station spacing cannot repair a static support deficit. The unchanged
canonical module produces 3,064 N at the retained +120 A point and weighs 4,275 N:
each added copy contributes **−1,211 N of net support** before carrying any body
or passengers. The canonical six-car train contains 144 such modules. Raising
their number does not create a levitating design.

This [executed study](support-architecture.json), generated by the
[independent research module](../../../../engineering/maglev_research/support_architecture.py),
keeps the complete canonical nonmodule AW3 inventory: **27,155.259 kg/car**,
including **12,000 kg/car passengers**. There is no unearned light-body credit.
It preserves the original archives and canonical configuration.

## Actual bounded model work

The 256 additional shared-circuit cases compare four pole widths, two core
scales, two guidance pack widths, retained versus newly counted cooling, original
versus sensitivity flux allocation, and 24/28/30/32 modules per car. Each exact
candidate feeds its new mass back into lift and 0.1 AW3 lateral demand. All retain
120 A, 3.75 A/mm², 55% copper fill, 120°C copper and 2.2 T field limits. The
separate 1.3 AW3 lift/0.1 AW3 lateral peak check remains mandatory.

| Screen | Passing cases | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Nominal joint current/field/density/thermal | 32 | All depend on the unvalidated sensitivity topology |
| Nominal joint screen and longitudinal array fit | 17 | Still missing adverse-load reserve |
| Nominal joint, 1.3 peak and array fit together | **0** | No simultaneous shared-circuit survivor |

The lightest nominal joint case that fits is **53.747 t AW3/car**, with 28 modules
and the expanded pack/counted cooler. Its 1.3 peak demands 229.8 A lift and
170.4 A guidance at 2.403 T, so it fails. It is not a completed train design.

Increasing module count also increases manufacturing and integration work:
24/28/30/32 modules per car mean 144/168/180/192 per six-car train. The exact
wide stock envelope is 0.96 m long; at 50 mm intermodule clearance and two rows,
32 modules require 16.11 m per row, exceeding the declared 16 m usable length
inside a 17 m body. Thirty fit this nominal longitudinal screen. Actual mounts,
corners, curves, cables, pipes and evacuation interfaces remain unqualified.

## Cooling is counted rather than free

The proposed dedicated guide cooler adds **20.923 kg/module**. It counts a
24-fin aluminium stock set, liquid tank and fluid, fan blades/hubs/housings,
motor steel/copper, pump shell/rotor/winding envelope, hoses and wires. The
original module coldplate is retained. It adds a **74.215 W/module auxiliary
power target**. Air/fluid convection, wetted fraction, flow, pressure and fan/pump
efficiency are explicit **unmeasured targets**, not component performance data.

Guide conductance is derived from the series resistance of coil/fluid transfer,
the tank wall, finite-efficiency fins, and bulk air heating. It is approximately
54–59 W/K in these cases. Raw aluminium/steel/copper stock is about $87.59 per
cooler at the retained unquoted planning rates; fabrication, polymer/fluid,
motors/drives, quotations, protection and qualification are not fully priced.

The 188→240 mm pack change recounts copper, enamel, fluid, bobbins and cover/
coldplate edge strips. Every extra support module also adds an explicit
**5.613 kg** wire/insulation/hose/fluid/plate takeoff. Current drives, complete
routing, cooling failure protection and structural redesign remain open stock
and qualification tasks; they do not receive zero-cost acceptance credit.

## Simpler separate-guidance candidate to investigate

The most useful next specification separates balanced lift from lateral control,
so a guidance demand no longer drives unequal flux through the shared lift core.
This changes the architecture and requires its own guidance field model. The
study therefore distinguishes **18 separate-guidance material/geometry bounds**
from the 256 executed shared-field model cases.

The next original-flux reference is `separate-guidance-03`: **30 support modules
plus six dedicated guidance U-core coupons per car**, three in each opposed
bank. It removes the shared guidance winding/bobbin stock, retains the complete
lift/core/PM stock, adds each new guide unit and its counted cooler, and solves
the resulting mass fixed point. The accounted result is a **54.469 t AW3/car
lower bound**, or **326.816 t for six cars**. Installed omissions can only raise
this comparison mass.

The original-flux symmetric support evaluator passes its conditional support
screens at that lower-bound mass: about −8.0 A nominal, 60.0 A and 2.157 T at
1.3 AW3, and a 40.40°C conditional symmetric copper equilibrium. **The new
guidance circuit has not been simulated**, so these support results are not a
simultaneous lift/guidance survivor.

These are **optimistic geometry/material bounds**, not operating ratings. The
2.2 T steel point and airgap-only MMF leave steel reluctance, fringing/leakage and
control margin unresolved. A practical operating field and paid MMF reserve must
be established by the independent nonlinear model. Protection, sensors, actual
turn routing, retention, manufacture, stationary reaction steel and installed
cooler/guide positions remain additional design work. Mean gap field is reduced
to 2.09 T for the 95% lamination fill; gap pressure uses the gross physical face
area, preserving the 2.2 T steel limit without taking an inconsistent fill credit.

This split has **36 wound coils/car versus the original 72**, a useful production
simplification to test. It also has more actuators and a substantially heavier
train. Fewer coils do not establish a lower complete installed price. Its mass
must be used when rerunning propulsion, charging, journey time, headway and
civil loads; the canonical 37.619 t/car operating comparison cannot be inherited.

The nominal suspension power subtotal is **5.957 kW/car**: 0.290 kW conditional
support copper, 4.957 kW guide copper at the 120°C bound, 0.445 kW for all six new
guide cooler auxiliaries and 0.265 kW for the 30 retained support-pump targets.
This consumes part of the existing **10 kW/car suspension allowance** in the
normal/hot services model; it must not be added to that allowance a second time.
About 4.043 kW remains for uncounted core/PWM/rail/converter losses, with no
qualified duty or power margin established.

At the static 1.3 load point, support copper alone is **16.305 kW/car** at 40°C
resistance; the same subtotal becomes **21.972 kW/car**, exceeding that allowance.
Duration, repeated duty, source reserve, protection and converter capability must
be established. A uniform 36-new-cooler option would require 2.672 kW/car of fan/
pump auxiliary targets and another 627.687 kg/car of stock; its mass feedback has
not been executed. The 54.469 t case counts six new guide coolers and retains
the existing support heat paths.

## Mass ceilings and next experiments

Every shared-circuit case retains an optimistic return/pole-area AW3 mass bound
and the corresponding nonmodule tare allowance. Those bounds include the
simultaneous lateral demand and 1.3 lift factor but omit leakage, current and
thermal restrictions; passing them is necessary, never sufficient. Pareto case
IDs retain nondominated mass/constraint/module-count tradeoffs separately for
the original and sensitivity allocations. No body stock or passenger payload
was removed to manufacture a passing result.

The next concrete work is an independent spatial U-core coupon with the above
counted winding window and opposite-bank force requirements, followed by a
lower-field/MMF-margin design and a manufactured force/thermal coupon. It must
include full extra reaction-rail, current-drive, sensor, retention, cooler and
hardpoint quantities. Only then can a single mass/configuration revision feed
the integrated six-car fault and four-stop simulation. No physical, engineering
or configuration-adoption gate is closed here.
