OpenSourceRail: open railway design and delivery

OpenSourceRail connects city planning, railway engineering, manufacturing, construction, business workflows and open train-control software. Its purpose is to build local design authority, production and repair capability while comparing whole-life cost and passenger service. These are project objectives; economic superiority and physical qualification have not been demonstrated.

Published v0.7.2 and current Baghdad review

The v0.7.2 Baghdad Airport release joins a 12 km, six-stop schematic, a 15-minute opening timetable, a six-set fleet, radial traction supply, a supplier dossier and a 2026 USD planning estimate. Its 2,800 normal places/hour/direction and $1,122.8 million central opening capital belong to that released configuration. Site surveys, equipment returns, physical tests and an owner decision remain necessary before construction or passenger service.

Current main-branch work tests a revised 17.46 km horizontal Airport route. The 30-minute paired-supply replay completes 72 of 72 booked trips under its declared model. Both evaluated supply layouts fail engineering screens, and Um Al Tubol still needs a site. The paired-supply budgetary enquiry is tied to that revised evaluation. The later first-article prototype retains revised car and workshop records on the earlier 12 km schematic; its drawings and $1.124 billion radial-layout estimate do not become the 17.46 km enquiry. Retained v0.6 development vehicle

The selected development vehicle reference has 6 cars of 17 m. Each car is planned for 44 fixed seats and 2 wheelchair places, with no planned standing. Passive steel-wheel bogies run on conventional rails carried by concrete guideway. The retained passive aluminium secondary is an interface reference; it does not select installed station LIM propulsion. The reference vehicle has no normal-service traction motor, traction battery or permanent magnet. Independent braking, service power, protection and rescue remain required.

Installed propulsion architecture, powered length, acceleration, braking and headway must be evaluated together for each candidate. Neither 900 m nor 100 m is a prescribed installation. The named retained service control uses 72 km/h launch speed, 120 seconds headway and 35 seconds dwell; these are reproducibility controls, not selected operating requirements. Its 8,280 passengers/hour/direction is capacity arithmetic, not a demonstrated protected service. The selected vehicle is a development reference without manufacturing or passenger-operation release.

Reference and alternative describe selection and comparison roles. Evaluated results retain their declared calculation and evidence scope. Prototype validation and release require separate physical evidence and review.

Planning, engineering and delivery

Use the current city catalogue and planning workspace to inspect candidate routes, access, service and costs. The current coordinated search starts with ./osr design railway, the coupled campaign and its candidate-specific evidence. Alternative architectures use their own physical, evaluation and selection identities. The source-bound public records support comparison and enquiry, not automatic engineering admission.

The following v0.6 planning and factory example remains a development workflow for the selected passive-wheel vehicle. Its quantities and costs do not carry over to the Airport or v0.7 powered-wheel candidates.

The planning workspace verifies city geography and route sources, then supports route, stop, service and OD proposals. Run ./osr plan compile edits.json --output build/my-plan to create an immutable handoff bound to the selected candidate; run ./osr plan verify build/my-plan to verify its source files and identities. The handoff contains proposals only. It excludes vehicle, propulsion, fleet, civil quantity, cost, performance and cashflow results; those require candidate-specific evaluation. Planning revisions do not grant movement authority.

From a verified handoff, create an immutable six-car factory work package with:

./osr twin factory-draft compile build/planning-handoff --output build/factory-plan
./osr twin factory-draft verify build/planning-handoff build/factory-plan

The draft uses the selected candidate's Class C cost ledger and source-linked low/base/high process hours under two 8-hour production shifts. It schedules one serialized planning lane and does not infer crew size, parallel capacity or calendar dates. Civil and railway-operations schedules, purchasing and manufacturing release remain held.

The coordinated design workflow propagates one candidate through geometry, mass, engineering analysis, BOM, CAD/IFC, manufacturing, procurement, construction and controls. The current export manifest and delivery package index bind research records to candidate identities. ERPNext exports are disabled drafts. Complete installed equipment, an available operating motor map, force-driven journeys and fully supported lifecycle costs remain open.

Business and operating software

ERPNext and Frappe HR support draft business, workforce, procurement, manufacturing, quality and maintenance workflows. FUXA and the gateway support equipment observations, alarms, history and review-linked records. Site masters, permissions, device bindings and reviewed imports require deployment-specific configuration. These services do not commission a railway or supply movement authority.

Cost scenarios use two 8-hour shifts for production and civil work. Operations are modelled at two shifts for 16/18-hour service and three shifts for a 24-hour case. These are planning assumptions, not approved staffing or operating plans.

Start here

Run ./install.sh, then ./osr status and ./osr workbench. Use ./osr design railway for coordinated candidate research and the commands in each named packet to reproduce its declared evaluation. ./osr verify checks available source-bound publications; it does not qualify hardware.
