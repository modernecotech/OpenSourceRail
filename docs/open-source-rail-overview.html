Fewer moving parts, local manufacture and compact elevated urban routes.

Fixed linear synchronous motors launch and receive the train at stations. Selected longer legs add located midway acceleration; remaining guideway is unpowered; onboard ferrite PM-biased active support, guidance and passenger services remain independently powered. Removing main traction equipment from the train shifts electrical plant to stations. Support coils/drives, cooling, sensing, protection, doors, HVAC and emergency equipment remain necessary. Contactless running aims to reduce contact noise and wear; actual losses, acoustic performance and maintenance require measurement.

Body, civil works and local capability

Baghdad uses six 17 m bodies. The central tare target is 14.2 t/car, including a 5.2 t equipped structural GFRP body, 3 t ferrite, 30 kWh services storage and an existing 1.8 t growth reserve. Four passenger doors per car are the body takeoff. Counted frame families, supported panels, couplers and hardpoint candidates now have calculated dimensions and margins; frame/emergency-brake growth consumes this reserve. Joined-body strength, complete equipment, fatigue, CG and inertia remain unqualified. The50kWh wider-corridor reference is 14.4t tare/ 26.4t AW3 per car. Its pack increase is included once; the primary detail/emergency screens use that heavier mass alongside the retained30kWh case.

The current ordinary civil candidate is a 25 m, 0.80 × 2.50 m closed concrete box with 100 mm webs, 150 mm flanges and end infills. The supplied 1.80 m-depth case remains a separate comparison. Hollow columns/caps and alternative foundation families are compared by quantity. Necessary passive iron, anchors, bearings, connections and final survey remain in scope. Ground-specific selection, prestress/handling details, fixed/guided bearings and emergency load paths have numerical screens; physical structural and construction acceptance remain open.

The research target omits a continuous external walkway. Vertical spiral chutes are the preferred alternative under investigation, without walkway-removal approval. Safe landing, assisted evacuation, deployment, stopping/recovery and fire response must work at every allowed stopping location.

Operating and cost boundaries

Urban passenger stops target at least 1.5 km spacing and about 2 km on average. Suburban stops target 4–5 km with fixed midway acceleration where required. Junctions transfer passengers between independent fixed lines, without train-routing switches. Retained close junctions remain spacing/access exceptions requiring a solution. Straight alignments are planning candidates subject to land, buildings, utilities, grades, access and evacuation.

The 1 km/88 s/180 s station and moving packets remain component benchmarks. The supplied 90 s/190 s case is a separate authored comparison. Longer urban/suburban profiles have their own finite-zone, power, services, clearance and actual Rust fault tests. A separate local multi-train prototype now executes progressive Rust reservations and stopping-distance envelopes. Capacity shortfalls and unqualified route-wide sectioned protection stay visible; a timer does not prove a clear railway.

The retained benchmark uses 240 kW/car charger input at 90% assumed conversion efficiency. Longer cases compare their own pack, charger and dwell choices. Independently equipped rescue nodes, faster winches and 30/50/60 kWh packs have explicit rope/drum/portal/foundation, drive/power, mass and cost comparisons. A midpoint accelerator is not automatically a refuge, charging station or rescue site. Rope/termination, access, essential loads, physical hardware and real ground remain unqualified.

City subtotals separately count eight scopes: fleet, ordinary and terminal civil work, station electrical targets, a conditional footing increment, booster electrical plant, booster mount stock and independent rescue kits. Complete station/transfer hubs, grid reinforcement, land, factory capital, evacuation, qualification, finance and other unpriced work remain additional. Fewer passenger platforms do not by themselves establish a total saving.

Baghdad interchange investigation

The default Baghdad plan remains 273 proposed stops with 10 transfer-spacing conflicts. A separate interchange investigation compares extended walking links on unchanged guideways with coordinated shared-site hub concepts that change guideway geometry. No alternative is selected.

Original boarding/access records remain traceable. The extended links retain reference walks and partial electrical target credits, without complete connector costs or ridership-weighted benefits. Shared-site concepts draw involved fixed lines through one location; the original100m compact walk is only a sensitivity. The separate geometry study below fits new approaches, powered zones, platforms and actual internal paths without selecting or qualifying a hub. Lift resilience, peak transfer pulses, shade, resting places, evacuation, land and complete construction/maintenance costs must enter selection. Trains never switch between lines in these passenger-transfer proposals.

The separate access engineering study adds physical connector/support quantities, accessible routes, passenger pulses and lift-outage comparisons. Assumed loads, flow rates and geometry remain provisional; no option is selected and no complete construction price is claimed.

The shared-hub geometry study develops new station tangents, both directional powered zones, bounded approach transitions, platforms and actual door-to-door paths. Rejected fits remain visible; new quantities receive no previous civil price credit. This separate study does not turn the original100m sensitivity into a measured transfer walk.

The longitudinal-stock sweep tests failure-directed alternatives after the GFRP stiffness hold. Exact drilled steel stock and changed spacers add their mass to the complete retained AW3 basis. The unchanged phase criterion retains passes and failures; thin-wall joints, heavier loads, qualified materials and flexible-body behavior remain open. No stock alternative changes the city default.

Requirement closure and physical evidence

The qualification programme gives each applicable requirement a deliverable, prerequisites and a route to actual test or acceptance evidence. Specimen/rig procedures and typed raw records cover materials, magnetic forces and losses, controls, civil stages, services, egress and recovery. Prepared procedures and synthetic records do not constitute physical tests. Qualified limits, exact revisions and authenticated independent decisions remain necessary.

The multi-train capacity study compares local progressive ownership with exclusive whole-leg protection and retains adverse cases. It does not promote city schedules or approve an operating railway. Portable study bundles now use bounded, checksum-verified numbered archives so that the complete retained source set can exceed one archive's size without dropping evidence.
