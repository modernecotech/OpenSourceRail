# Viaduct Bearing And Movement Schedule

Compare two twin-track schemes over the same four-span movement unit.
Bearings remain replaceable in both. The reduced arrangement is an unaccepted
structural alternative, not a saving established by a link slab.

| Support | Tracks | Bearing lines | Bearings |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Abutment/end support | 2 | 1 | 4 |
| Internal pier, structural continuity connection | 2 | 1 | 4 |
| Internal pier, simple spans with link slab | 2 | 2 | 8 |
| Expansion-unit boundary | 2 | 2 | 8 |

Internal bearings are in one longitudinal row; adjacent unit ends retain two
independent rows at an expansion boundary. The supplier schedule must
assign fixed, guided, and free directions for every span end and show design
reactions, translations, rotations, uplift restraint if required, replacement
loads, installation temperature, and identification marks. Permanent jacking
shelves and a checked load path are mandatory.

The 25 m reference places a deck gap every four spans, nominally 100 m; the
permitted four/five-span family stays within 80–125 m. A structural connection
must establish the shared support load path, including construction stages,
prestress, reinforcement and fatigue. A link slab may remove a deck joint while
each girder retains its independent end bearings and permitted rotation.
For the periodic 25 m reference kilometre these alternatives require 200 and
320 bearings respectively. A finite 40-span structural layout also has 200
bearings: 30 internal supports, nine boundaries and two end supports. It has
41 supports and nine expansion gaps; the periodic index has 40 supports and ten
gaps. Do not mix periodic and finite corridor quantities.
The project schedule must close thermal,
creep, shrinkage, seismic, braking, bearing-friction, connection fatigue and
differential-settlement actions; provide covers over walkways and cable
routes; and complete continuous-welded-rail/bridge interaction. Rail expansion
devices are installed only where that analysis requires them.

The [FHWA UHPC connection guidance](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/publications/research/infrastructure/structures/14084/index.cfm)
describes a link slab over simple-span girders with rotating/moving bearings.
It is a highway precedent, not a released rail design. Connection cost,
foundation forces, maintenance access and bearing replacement must accompany
both bids before whole-life selection.
