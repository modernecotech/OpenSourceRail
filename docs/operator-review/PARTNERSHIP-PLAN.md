# How to secure a first operating partner and sponsor

**First decision requested:** a prospective owner names a decision-maker, a prospective operator names a planner, and both agree to a two-week trial using one local transport problem. Bring one service or asset record they have permission to share. At the end they can fund a bounded study, adopt the software alone, request changes or stop. A city can use this process with its own corridor or depot.

This plan follows a review of main at `97adb7e5c3`. The appointments, budget and response targets below are proposals. The existing twelve-week engineering programme starts once a sponsor and access to records are in place. Use its route, factory and commissioning tasks when the partner commissions them. ## Who needs to decide, and what to ask

Roles below are invitations. The maintainer identifies a convenor for the exploratory meeting. The owner appoints the delivery leads if it funds the study. Each organisation retains its own decision rights.

| Stakeholder and concern | First exchange | Observable commitment or reason to revise |
|---|---|---|
| City sponsor, transport authority and budget holder — a useful service at an affordable annual cost | Ask which journeys are underserved, which budget pays for service, and who can authorise data access. Bring a one-page comparison with existing service, an improved bus or interchange option, and the proposed rail service. | Sponsor names a decision owner, documents the public objective, sets a spending ceiling and dates the review. If a bus or software intervention meets the objective, take it forward on its merits. |
| Operator, depot staff and workforce representatives — service recovery, shifts and repair | Let a planner reproduce a timetable conflict or a maintainer trace a failed part to an inspection and repair record. Ask staff to identify a missing task, competence or handover. | A practitioner repeats the task independently, records the time and errors against current practice, and identifies an owner and training need for a second trial. |
| Passengers, disability organisations, bus and taxi users, neighbouring residents and traders — access, fares, disruption and livelihoods | Run an accessible walk or map session at a proposed entrance and interchange. Ask about journeys missed, assisted boarding, heat, fares, deliveries, noise and night working. For Baghdad offer Arabic materials, an offline or telephone response and participant access support. | Return a dated account of what changed, what remains disputed and why. Track travel time including transfers, step-free access gaps and disruption by location. Record dissent separately. |
| Land, road, utility and emergency-service owners — practical access and safe recovery | Review one proposed yard, one difficult crossing and the evacuation or isolation route. Request mapped exclusions, access windows and a named data custodian. | Each affected owner identifies feasible conditions, further investigation or a conflict. Rework the comparison if a yard, feed or rescue route is unavailable. |
| Local precasters, fabricators, installers, training providers and competing vendors — credible volumes, tooling ownership and local work | Send the same revision to competing shops. Ask for delivered price, lead time, exclusions, minimum volume, existing plant, training, inspection and repair rights. Invite a challenge to the proposed method. | Buyer records valid returns by lot, quote expiry and unmatched scope. Local hours, origin and tooling are evidenced separately. A single response can inform a budget and is disclosed as single-source. [Return procedure](../../cities/Baghdad/engineering/simulation/audit-production-guide.md) |
| Treasury, development funders and lenders — affordability and exposure to overruns | Share phased cash, operating support, renewals, incomplete scope and the downside case. Ask which risks they would carry and what evidence is needed for a preparation grant or facility. | Written eligibility feedback, a ceiling or indicative terms with conditions, responsible payer and expiry. Count committed funding only on written authority. [Finance workshop](finance/README.md) |
| Safety authority, independent assessor, universities and test labs — evidence they can examine independently | Ask the authority to identify its process and the assessor to scope one review, such as degraded stopping or feeder protection. Offer a university or lab a reproducible case and agree what it will measure. | Obtain a review scope, independence declaration, evidence owner and estimate for the next test. Record disagreements and their consequences for the design. [Compliance crosswalk](../certification/compliance-matrix.md) |
| Owner's IT team, local integrators and open-source maintainers — continuity if a supplier leaves | Install the planning or asset workflow on a partner-controlled machine. Export, restore and revise one record from a pinned edition. Identify support, data ownership, licences and handover skills. | Partner retains a usable export, records installation and recovery issues, and names a local administrator or declines deployment. ## Work programme and spending decisions

The clock starts with a willing host. The convenor can prepare public materials beforehand. The sponsor selects the corridor and comparison service. The operator can pursue the software track even when the infrastructure option stops.

| When and who leads | Work and deliverable | Decision at the end |
|---|---|---|
| First five working days · convenor | Prepare a one-page brief stating the local problem, beneficiaries, current alternative, unresolved question, requested person or dataset and decision date. Attach the operator kit and one relevant map or film. Identify the budget holder, host, affected groups and people missing from the room. | A host agrees to a trial date and nominates a practitioner. Without a host, record the objection and change the offer before commissioning analysis. |
| Weeks 1–2 · host operator and convenor | Run the offline workshop. Use its final ten minutes for the partner to change an input, explain the result and name a decision it changes. Collect permissions for one local dataset, the comparison measure and a short pilot charter. | Sponsor signs or declines the charter with scope, staff time, cost ceiling, data custodian, success measure and decision date. A software-only host can proceed with the workflow trial independently. |
| Weeks 3–4 · passenger/access lead and operator | Collect a small, time-and-place-labelled baseline of counts, fares, wait and transfer times. Document gaps and who is excluded. Run the user and street-access sessions. Run the software pilot twice, with partner staff leading the repeat. | Host accepts, revises or stops the workflow pilot. Planning lead freezes the comparison brief or requests more observations if the sample cannot support it. |
| Weeks 5–8 · owner's engineer, buyer and independent reviewer | Test the shortlisted options against the same demand, hours, accessibility and cost boundary. Issue two budget enquiries, for example erection and feeder protection. Compare current and revised assumptions and review one governing failure independently. | Return a revision-controlled options table, comparable offers or refusals, an open-risk register and a priced investigation for each decision-changing unknown. Hold any affected option with an unresolved mandatory constraint. |
| Weeks 9–12 · finance lead and sponsor | Review options with affected users, publish responses, and reconcile annual buyer cash with priced and open scope. Prepare a two-page decision paper covering service need, alternatives and benefits, supplier capacity, affordability and accountable delivery. Attach the evidence and dissent. | Sponsor records a dated choice: adopt the digital workflow, commission a specified survey or bench trial, request redesign, or stop. A limited study order states its deliverable, maximum spend and review date. |
| Following quarter · commissioning organisation | Carry only authorised work into the delivery workplan. Verify any new observations or quotes against the candidate, rerun affected studies, and report the effect to contributors. | Renew, expand or close the pilot on measured use, cost and findings. A physical demonstrator needs its own site, funding, test limits and approvals. |

**Illustrative cost of the twelve-week engagement.** To establish a ceiling, allow 15 convenor days, 12 operator/planner days, 10 community and accessibility days, 14 engineering and procurement days, 6 finance days and 5 independent review days. At an assumed blended USD 500 per person-day, 62 days cost USD 31,000. Allow USD 6,000 for local travel, accessible participation, interpreting and meeting logistics, giving **USD 37,000**. At USD 350–750 per day with those expenses held fixed, the range is USD 27,700–52,500. These are illustrative planning inputs. Obtain local rates and price access support before approval. Identify partner time contributed in kind and credit staff already funded in the delivery workplan. Separate taxes, formal surveys, lab or rig hire, fabrication and construction into their own quotes. Limit the initial two weeks to eight of the 62 days plus up to USD 1,000 of the expenses, or **USD 5,000 at the base rate**, before seeking the balance.

## How the sponsor can tell whether support is real

The convenor keeps one decision log. For each entry record the organisation and role, decision requested, revision reviewed, evidence received, objection, owner, due date, next decision and permission to attribute. Distinguish invited, attended, contributed evidence, agreed in writing, funded and declined. Publish aggregate responses and consented decisions. Keep personal travel data, commercially sensitive offers and identifying feedback with the responsible custodian. Provide a way to raise a concern privately and a date by which the convenor will respond.

| Review measure | Proposed target or trigger |
|---|---|
| Mandate and use | By week 2, one host with a named practitioner and a dated charter. By week 4, one partner-led repeat with time, errors and a written adopt/change/stop verdict. |
| Representation and response | Invite affected users and operators directly, including disabled passengers. By week 4 document missing voices. Before the sponsor vote, give every material concern a written response, an owner or a reason it remains disputed. |
| Commercial credibility | Seek two comparable returns for each priority lot where a market exists. Report firms approached, valid returns, unpriced scope and exclusions. If comparison fails, commission a revised enquiry or retain the estimate range. |
| Public value and affordability | Compare journey time, access, disruption, local repair capability, annual public support and renewal exposure on equal scope. Let the sponsor state its affordability ceiling before seeing the ranking. |
| Authority to proceed | At week 12 identify the signer, authorised work, money and conditions. Carry unanswered safety, land and funding questions into the decision record. Participation, a logo or a workshop attendance count confers no endorsement. |

Use the existing workshop for the briefing, the buyer's return schedule for prices, and the standards register for technical evidence. The decision log links to those records. The convenor reviews overdue actions weekly and returns a short “you raised / we changed / still open” note after each workshop. Invite local review of the wording before publishing a partner's name or position.

The five questions in the sponsor paper draw on UK transport business-case guidance. The emphasis on an accountable spokesperson and early participation draws on the World Bank's stakeholder engagement guidance. These are methods a local sponsor can adapt under its own procurement and approval arrangements.
