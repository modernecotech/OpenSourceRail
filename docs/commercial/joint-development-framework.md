# Joint Development And Delivery Framework

This framework turns a possible industrial partnership into evidence-bearing
work. The recommended first instrument is a **funded development agreement with
a defined route to an equity joint venture**, not an immediate broad partnership
announcement or permanent territorial exclusivity.

> This is a technical and commercial readiness framework, not legal, tax,
> procurement, financing or investment advice. Local counsel and the relevant
> customer/authority must select and approve the actual structure.

## Proposed Purpose

Develop and deliver affordable urban-rail products and owner/operator systems
with open engineering information, qualified specialist equipment,
progressively capable local production and continuing support.

The business can earn revenue before a complete railway is available:

| Offer | Customer receives |
|---|---|
| Engineering development | Bounded studies, requirements, reference adaptation, design packages and procurement preparation |
| Owner/operator platform | Configured asset, work, procurement, competence, maintenance and assurance workflows |
| Prototype/pilot delivery | Equipment, integration, local assembly and an agreed test programme |
| Vehicle/module supply | Configuration-controlled accepted products, spares and support |
| Factory and workforce preparation | Tooling, methods, qualification, training and competence transfer |
| Long-term support | Maintenance assistance, software support, engineering changes and fleet expansion |

Non-recurring development, factory/tooling, first-of-class acceptance and
repeat-production equipment remain separate prices.

## Three-Layer Structure

| Layer | Default form | Retained purpose |
|---|---|---|
| OpenSourceRail project | Public development under the existing software, hardware/design and documentation licences | Maintain the reusable public platform, evidence and governance |
| Commercial development/delivery venture | Contractual collaboration first; an equity entity only after accepted commitments justify it | Employ engineers, fund work, contract, integrate, support and carry allocated commercial responsibility |
| Country delivery/manufacturing | Customer contracts and local workshops first; local subsidiary/JV when funded volume justifies it | Perform site-specific delivery, develop local capability and maintain the railway |

The local railway owner is normally the customer and asset owner, not merely a
sales channel. It retains its statutory duties, requirements, project records
and acceptance decisions.

## Counterparty Qualification

A brand, website relationship or product catalogue is not manufacturer backing.
Before negotiation passes gate P0, record:

- the exact legal entity becoming a party or shareholder;
- registration, beneficial ownership and authority to commit;
- named engineers, facilities and availability;
- each manufacturer it is contractually authorised to represent;
- who owns and can license each drawing, software item and method;
- who warrants the delivered configuration and carries product liability;
- conflicts, commissions, related parties and sanctions/export-control review;
- the proposed procurement role: owner adviser, prospective supplier or
  integrated contractor.

RailMac describes itself as a SinoMac sub-brand and states that SinoMac seeks
country partners through branches or joint ventures. That makes a proposal
plausible, but is not evidence that RailMac, SinoMac, CRRC or any named
manufacturer has committed resources to OpenSourceRail. See the current
[RailMac site](https://rail-mac.com/) and require direct contractual evidence.

## Contribution Before Equity

Negotiate ownership from accepted value and continuing obligations rather than
from an assumed 50:50 or 51:49 split.

Every contribution records:

1. contributing legal party;
2. cash, people, equipment, facility, technology/right or customer/site input;
3. bounded quantity, timing and valuation method;
4. acceptance criteria and non-conflicted acceptance owner;
5. evidence of delivery and treatment if it is late, defective or withdrawn;
6. whether the JV pays separately for the same item.

Future support promises do not count as delivered equity. Equipment invoiced at
full commercial price is not also an in-kind equity contribution. The public
code is already non-exclusive; commercial value comes from maintained
configurations, engineering capacity, qualified production, implementation,
support and accepted references.

Use the machine-readable
[`commercial-partnership-readiness.toml`](../../lib/templates/commercial-partnership-readiness.toml)
as the contribution and decision register. The tracked reference remains blank;
confidential copies live in the controlled opportunity workspace.

## Governance And Reserved Matters

Routine work follows an approved budget and management delegation. Joint or
shareholder approval should be confined to material matters such as:

- purpose, localisation commitments and approved business plan;
- disposal or exclusive licensing of material technology, data, tooling or brand;
- major borrowing, guarantees, new shares and dilution;
- large or unusual related-party purchases;
- entry into a materially different business, sale or winding-up;
- changes to owner continuity, open-platform or alternative-supplier rights.

The shareholders' agreement needs a practical deadlock, capital-shortfall,
default, withdrawal, valuation, transfer and termination route. Majority
ownership alone does not solve these issues.

## IP, Data And Manufacturing Rights

[WIPO](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/technology-transfer/agreements) describes
equity and contractual JVs and recommends early agreement on background and
new IP. The project therefore separates six records:

| Material | Required treatment |
|---|---|
| Existing public OpenSourceRail work | Existing licences remain effective; it is not converted into an exclusive asset |
| Supplier background technology | Used only under rights the supplier is authorised to grant |
| New reusable development | Ownership, publication, licence, maintenance and patent treatment agreed before work starts |
| Customer-specific information | Delivery, access, confidentiality, retention, security and exit rights defined |
| Brands/trademarks | Ownership and permitted use separate from copyright licences |
| Manufacturing information | Editable drawings, tooling data, processes, inspection/diagnostics and training usable for the agreed local work and continuity |

Apache-2.0 allows commercial services and separates trademark rights. CERN-OHL-S
v2 is a strongly reciprocal hardware licence. Component boundaries and available
component treatment must be reviewed before public hardware definitions and
supplier proprietary material are combined. See the [Apache licence](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0)
and [CERN OHL source](https://cern-ohl.web.cern.ch/).

Technology transfer is complete only when named local people can perform the
agreed task with the delivered information, tooling, rights, inspection method
and authorization—not when a folder of PDFs changes hands.

## Shareholder-Supplier Controls

An industrial shareholder may also supply equipment. The venture therefore
requires:

- agreed related-party pricing and benchmarkable major quotations;
- disclosure of rebates, commissions and conflicts;
- non-conflicted approval and audit rights;
- exact configuration, acceptance, warranty and liability allocation;
- open-book treatment of development versus repeat-unit charges;
- the right to qualify alternatives against objective performance;
- step-in, data, tooling, spares and termination assistance.

Default to non-exclusive cooperation. Any exclusivity must name territories or
opportunities, have a finite term, measurable investment/delivery/support
conditions and termination rights. There is no permanent blanket exclusivity
for the “developing world” in the reference template.

## Separate The Two Financing Problems

| Capital need | Pays for | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Venture/development capital | Engineers, prototypes, tests, customer development and working capital | Shareholder/customer commitments, budget, cash controls and contribution acceptance |
| Railway project finance | Land, civil works, stations, fleet, commissioning and operating mobilisation | Customer/project approval, procurement strategy, affordability, finance and risk allocation |

Formation of a JV does not finance a railway. The first venture contract should
not combine unproven product-development, construction, demand, currency and
long-term fare risk unless the parties explicitly price, finance and allocate
them.

Where development-bank procurement may apply, decide the venture's role before
paid preparation begins. Owner-side consultancy, prospective supply and an
integrated design-build/turnkey role have different conflict implications. The
[World Bank September 2025 procurement regulations](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/c84273d1b230aeb2b0b8134de5dc8cd7-0290012025/original/Procurement-Regulations-7th-Edition-Sep-2025.pdf)
are one relevant reference; the actual financing institution and procurement
documents govern the project.

## Localisation As Accepted Capability

| Stage | Capability | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|
| LOC-1 — Assembly and maintenance | Assemble selected equipment, inspect it, diagnose faults and replace modules | Authorized people, completed assemblies, inspection and maintenance demonstrations |
| LOC-2 — Selected fabrication | Produce suitable structures, panels, interiors, brackets and harnesses | Released drawings/fixtures, qualified processes and accepted first articles |
| LOC-3 — Broader production | Repeat additional assemblies when capability and volume justify it | Conformity, cost, delivery and qualified-supplier records |
| LOC-4 — Continuing engineering | Evaluate change, control configuration and qualify replacements | Competent roles, controlled records and accepted decisions |

Measure local payroll and invoices, origin evidence, tasks performed without
overseas intervention, supplier qualifications, first-pass yield, rework,
lead-time and cost trends. Contract labels or local final assembly alone are not
proof of design capability.

Alstom reports that its Gibela programme combines a 600-train customer order,
local manufacturing, long support/spares obligations and more than 65% local
content. The transferable lesson is that localisation was attached to a defined
customer programme and measurable industrial obligations—not that its volume or
percentage applies to OSR. See [Alstom's South Africa programme](https://www.alstom.com/alstom-south-africa).

## Reference Candidate Portfolio

Maintain an open-ended portfolio rather than limiting the venture to one example.
Candidates can include integrated corridors, existing workshop/depot deployments,
manufacturing first articles, supplier-neutral brownfield integrations and other
customer-defined pilots. Iraq is a logical source of initial examples because
repository evidence exists for Samawah and Mosul, but the repository contains no
customer order for either city.

Screen any number of potential candidates in parallel. Select a candidate for a
funded project only when it has:

1. an identifiable customer and decision-maker;
2. a funded bounded scope;
3. a usable site, workshop or corridor;
4. a defined engineering, procurement and approval route;
5. evidence reusable by later projects.

Possible packages include a non-safety owner/workshop deployment, an independently
compared corridor, supplier review of an LM3 first article, or a combination.
Several may proceed where each has its own customer, funding, scope, evidence and
approval route. Evidence is never pooled in a way that makes one candidate appear
ready because another passed.

The machine-readable template includes four illustrative screening rows and
accepts additional `[[reference_candidate]]` entries without a fixed maximum.
Once selected, each project receives a controlled project-specific copy so
confidential records and decisions remain separated.

## First 90 Days

| Period | Work | Required decision evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–30 | Qualify legal parties, authority, named resources, candidate customers and proposed contributions | P0 counterparty/opportunity record, candidate portfolio and draft heads of terms |
| Days 31–60 | Review LM3/product scope, supplier authority, component information, local facility and production tasks | Design-review findings, supplier-backed BOM/interface gaps and prototype/localisation plan |
| Days 61–90 | Price development, allocate responsibility, separate funding, agree IP/data/manufacturing rights and candidate propositions | Costed development agreement and board basis to form, defer or reject an equity JV and select zero or more funded candidates |

The 90 days define and fund work. They do not promise a production-ready or
approved train.

## Gated Decision Sequence

| Gate | Decision |
|---|---|
| P0 | Are the real counterparties, customer problem and procurement role verified? |
| P1 | Is a costed development agreement funded with contributions and IP treatment accepted? |
| P2 | Do delivered commitments justify forming/capitalising an equity JV? |
| P3 | Is at least one bounded customer-funded candidate project ready for authorization in its own record? |
| P4 | Do audited outcomes justify replication or wider territorial rights? |

Current public status is generated at
[commercial partnership readiness](partnership-readiness.md). It deliberately
shows zero accepted contributions and zero accepted gates until real evidence is
entered in a controlled deployment copy.
