# British supplier options for Baghdad factory equipment

British suppliers merit a package-by-package competition for the Baghdad facilities, particularly in specialist machining, composite processes, prestressing, lifting and inspection. The practical starting point is Iraqi construction and fabrication combined with selected imported process equipment, subject to a local capability survey. A British turnkey offer should be compared against that route, rather than assumed to be the cheapest or most supportable option.

Assessment date: **15 October 2026**. The [37-case matrix](baghdad-uk-factory-suppliers-2026-10-15.csv) distinguishes published UK manufacture, UK businesses supplying foreign equipment, international manufacturers with UK factories, and unresolved sourcing cases. These are public capability leads: prices, delivery commitments, Baghdad service arrangements and OSR compatibility require supplier returns. No supplier has been contacted.

## What counts as a British supply route

**UK manufacture published** means the cited supplier describes relevant UK manufacturing capability. It does not establish the offered machine's certified origin, every component's origin, local content percentage or ownership nationality. A supplier such as Mazak can have a UK factory while other machines in its range come from elsewhere.

**UK supply or integration** means a British business could supply engineering, commissioning or equipment. It does not make a foreign OEM machine British-made. XYZ's history records Taiwanese imports; Mills' catalogue describes distribution into the UK and Ireland. Their Iraq sales authority and overseas warranty must be established for the proposed transaction.

**UK and overseas manufacture** requires a factory declaration for each offer. FG Wilson identifies manufacturing in Northern Ireland and other countries; Wilson Power Solutions identifies English and Indian facilities and another OEM supply relationship. Brand address alone cannot allocate origin or financing.

The UK scope includes **Northern Ireland**. Origin records should identify the actual factory location. The Republic of Ireland is a separate country and is not counted as a British source here.

## Iraq delivery and British export finance

UKEF's [country cover table](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/country-cover-policy-and-indicators), updated 13 October 2026, lists Iraq with a **GBP 1–2 billion market risk appetite**, medium/long-term cover available for consideration, short-term cover requiring contact, and a location condition. This is a country-level indication, not money allocated or approval for the Baghdad plant.

The [UK content policy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ukefs-approach-to-foreign-content/ukefs-approach-to-foreign-content) includes a standard 20% UK-content approach and other transaction/project/supply-chain approaches. A UK invoice alone does not turn foreign machinery into qualifying UK content; engineering and services need a defensible allocation and UKEF agreement.

For the [Buyer Credit Facility](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/buyer-credit-facility), published conditions include a contract of at least GBP 5 million, an acceptable lending bank and repayment over at least two years. The loan can cover at most 85% of contract value, with at least 15% paid directly by the buyer before repayment starts. Product eligibility, premium and terms require a transaction assessment.

Prepare a genuine package of eligible machinery and services for comparison with commercial finance and the existing funding cases. Keep four quantities separate: **physical country of manufacture, qualifying UK content, value imported into Iraq, and Iraqi installation/local work**. They are not interchangeable percentages. The existing Chinese-credit and USD/IQD assumptions must be rerun as an explicit financing scenario if the supply chain changes.

Obtain a written export classification and licensing assessment for every offered model, including controls/software and applicable foreign-origin restrictions. The [UK Iraq sanctions guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/iraq-sanctions-guidance/iraq-sanctions-guidance) covers trade restrictions, strategic licensing overlap and Iraq-specific transit considerations. It is not shipment clearance.

The Iraqi importer and customs broker should confirm the current HS classification, duties/taxes, conformity requirements, origin/invoice authentication and route for each package. Do not apply a generic duty rate or US-export documentation procedure to a British shipment. Survey port/road handling, containerisation or out-of-gauge transport, site unloading and machine foundations before accepting a delivered price.

## Comparable supplier returns

Use the existing [supplier technical-support package](supplier-technical-support-package.md), especially manufacturing, localisation, first-article and lifecycle support work packages. A research row is not an accepted supplier record. Transfer an eventual controlled return into the existing intake and configuration process.

Request the following for each case:

1. **Offer and origin:** contracting entity, OEM authority, exact model/configuration, manufacturing factory, component origins, UK-content statement, Iraq sales permission and export responsibilities.
2. **Process proof:** supplied sample parts/materials, measured quality/yield and cycle data, fixtures/programs, inspection method, included tooling and utilities. State whether the offered capacity includes loading, inspection, maintenance and rejects.
3. **Installed cost:** equipment, tooling, software, packing/freight, insurance, duties/taxes, foundations/services, unloading, installation, commissioning, training, qualification, spares and recurring licences. Record currency, quotation validity and payment milestones separately.
4. **Baghdad support:** named service entity, response arrangements, critical spare stock, calibrated service capability, operator/maintenance training, manuals, controller backups and rights to maintain the equipment. UK-wide aftercare is not evidence of Baghdad coverage.
5. **Acceptance and lifecycle:** agreed factory acceptance tests and site acceptance tests, responsible witnesses, trial material, failure/rework disposition, warranties valid in Iraq, software access and replacement/obsolescence support.

For used or refurbished equipment, require condition and service records, controller support, missing guards/tooling, machine geometry checks and a priced refurbishment/acceptance scope. Its purchase price alone is not the comparison.

## Order of investigation

Start with the first-article process and inspection package: plate cutting, appropriately sized CNC, welding fixtures, metrology, material/NDT tests, composite tooling/process trials and engineered lifting. Obtain parallel technical returns from the precast batching, mould and stressing suppliers for one representative full-size package.

Next compare the complete UK rail battery option with licensed Baghdad pack assembly, and specify the winding and dynamometer route only against an approved motor process. Keep the full glazing line and heavy serial automation on hold until their revised make/buy cases and measured cycles support them.

Order duplicated cells, serial moulds and multiple erection machines against qualified output and accepted site demand. Public lead times, generic production claims and the planned 18-month readiness window do not establish a supplier-backed installation programme.
