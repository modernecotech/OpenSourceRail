# v0.6 functionality reconciliation against v0.4 and v0.5

The release-to-release comparison is anchored to the v0.6 source snapshot at `1ebfaa56b136fe61a68c6062f5d4c72373b77688`, the v0.4.0 release (`205e8dc158c83fcdc2dca9ceb8bad0a7e8b3f61e`), and the v0.5.1 release (`2617b04e722ca24b7ad9120f94610fe869f27053`). The v0.5.2 development record covers workflows added after that release. Follow-up work since the v0.6 snapshot is recorded below; retained code or historical packets are not counted as current, integrated v0.6 capability unless explicitly stated.

## v0.6 cleanup and restored entry points

- `./osr design --help` and `./osr plan --help` now show command guidance instead of falling through to a status/error record.
- The planning editor’s engineering link now opens the v0.6 Workbench. Historical headway and 1,500 m spacing screens are labelled as comparisons from the retained city packet, not current v0.6 requirements.
- The planning compiler emits only location, stop, dwell, headway and OD proposals, with their source hashes and current candidate identities. It does not copy v0.5 vehicles, powered guideways, fleet, costs, quantities, operating outcomes or release states.
- `./osr plan verify DIR` checks the planning package, edit bundle, retained geography inputs, selected v0.6 candidate and source hashes before review.
- The compile/verify regression covers the command-line handoff and confirms that a changed geography source is refused; the output keeps performance and complete cost unevaluated.
- The retained Trip Workbench is reconnected to its existing bounded loopback research service. Its magnetic vehicle, loads and 180-second service comparison remain separate from the selected v0.6 physical candidate and service target.
- Current engineering, movement-authority, procurement and physical-release dispositions remain false. The current selected reference and city-performance hold are unchanged.

The removed v0.4 applications run only from an isolated historical checkout. Their Rust package namespace and exact v0.4 data remain separate from the current v0.6 workspace, while current route proposals continue to use `./osr plan`. CI checks the restored City Studio and simulator/OCC interfaces separately. Use `./osr legacy checkout v0.4.0 --path /absolute/path` to create that checkout. The pre-comfort archive can be regenerated only from its exact source revision with `./osr legacy checkout pre-comfort-default --path /absolute/path`. The former v0.5 entry points (`capabilities`, `catalogue`, city packet generation, Baghdad simulation and PM-maglev adaptive iteration) can likewise be run from the formal `v0.5.1` tag or the later `v0.5-maglev` research snapshot using the same command. The current aliases remain read-only or deliberately held. Keep historical outputs in their own worktree and do not merge magnetic city quantities, train masses, energy or cost records into the wheel/LIM candidate.

## Work still required to restore the wider platform

At the compared `1ebfaa56` sourcing snapshot there were 100 immutable offline observations. The first v0.6 sourcing extension raised that to 290 records; subsequent 2026 updates added statistical material inputs, wage observations and industrial discovery leads. The current offline catalogue has **513 records**: 79 sources, 16 products, five published catalogue offers, three FX observations and 410 benchmarks. It contains September 2026 CSO governorate wage observations normalized from an explicit eight-hour day and additional Iraqi fabrication leads for foundry/forging, railway sleepers, prestressed beams, reinforcement mesh, plastics/chemicals, steel, cable, aluminium and ready-mix concrete. These are statistics and discovery leads, not supplier offers, verified operating capacity or railway-qualified parts. The official CSO 2024 private-building report retains 14 derived cost-to-quantity ratios; its source-bound historical cross-check applies the 2025 Baghdad base wage to the same recipe at 2/4/8 assumed hours, giving IQD 103,200/125,700/170,700 with the requested 20% uplift. The 2025 material comparator remains IQD 114,720/137,220/182,220. These are statistical construction estimates, not transactions; normal grit is not assumed equivalent to crushed gravel. The [current sourcing dossier](../design-engine/sourcing/README.md) and [validation index](../design-engine/sourcing/validation.json) retain source, method and packet identities. The federated purchasing roll-up covers 31,989 physical scopes in 7,042 sourcing requirements: 2,269 source-declared manufacturing groups retain 27,216 located occurrences, while 4,773 unlocated accounts remain separate. The balanced evaluation covered 137 requirements, with 6,905 pending, zero compatible priced matches and nine unsent RFQ drafts. No supplier communication was sent, and commercial order quantities remain unknown. The expanded source layer does not relax identity, scope, quantity or specification matching.

A candidate-bound material takeoff partitions all 27,216 located occurrences across those 2,269 manufacturing groups and preserves the 4,773 unlocated accounts separately. It derives 4,780.58 t of gross model stock, including 4,512.02 t / 1,804.81 m³ of nominal concrete stock. Copper and aluminium totals receive dated World Bank raw-commodity comparisons only; those values are not component prices or cost bounds. The September 2026 concrete recipe is mapped to all 81 concrete groups and 486 occurrences. With September Iraqi-origin material statistics and governorate base wages, its 2/4/8-hour screens are IQD 113,580/136,080/181,080 per nominal m³ in Baghdad and IQD 109,080/127,080/163,080 in Al-Muthanna. Applied to the research takeoff, the six values are IQD 204.990/245.598/326.815 million and IQD 196.869/229.355/294.328 million, respectively. The separate 2025 and July 2026 series remain separately dated comparators. These are research-candidate material-plus-labour scenarios with an unverified mix and assumed hours, not finished-beam or installed costs. The takeoff is still not a complete procurement BOM because most commercial units, process hours, yields and compatible finished-product offers remain absent.

The practical-launch comparison now applies a common 1.5 km / 2 km route matrix to 12 propulsion concepts, for 288 analytical required-demand and necessary-bound cases under the same vehicle, loading, speed, acceleration, grade, dwell and headway assumptions. Its 2 km 144-case view remains available for older consumers. The fixed-grip one-train-per-pitch cable bound falls short of 8,280 pphpd at both distances (about 6,551 at 1.5 km and 5,624 at 2 km). Available propulsion maps, electrical/mechanical losses, cooling, independent stopping and recovery, peak grid demand, complete installed quantities and protected fleet schedules remain open, so this is not a force-driven journey or preferred propulsion selection. The 900 m shared winding and five 20 m separated-bank study retain separate geometries and BOM scopes. The station-accelerator comparison now also carries a fail-closed bank-by-bank contract for the five 20 m study: five separately identified 20 m banks, 18 required-demand contexts, and explicit nulls for force maps, winding/electrical configuration, current and voltage, losses, heat rejection, repeated duty, mass and cost. It preserves required force demand as a requirement without presenting it as available thrust; the separate moving-LIM calculation is not transferred into the bank study. This documents the missing physical inputs and does not qualify or prefer the five-bank arrangement.

A Baghdad rebar cut/bend screen uses the 2025 regional material and wage reference plus declared labour hours and the requested 20% unallocated uplift. At 4, 8 and 16 skilled-worker hours per input tonne, the estimates are IQD 1,296,600/t, IQD 1,341,600/t and IQD 1,431,600/t. A separately dated September 2026 material observation produces IQD 1,231,800/t, IQD 1,276,800/t and IQD 1,366,800/t at the same assumed hours. The July 2026 Iraqi-origin rebar statistic gives IQD 1,203,000/1,248,000/1,338,000 per input tonne in Baghdad and IQD 1,194,000/1,230,000/1,302,000 in Al-Muthanna at 4/8/16 assumed hours, including 20%. The labour cycles are planning assumptions, not measured factory routes; rebar is not yet cost-mapped to the railway takeoff. The new aluminium extension maps Ur Company posted profile/sheet rates to two exact candidate stock rollups, with each of seven product forms kept separate and 8/16/32 assumed labour hours per tonne. Its formula is `(source material amount + assumed base-wage labour) × 1.20`: the `aluminium_reference` 17,535.607 kg rollup spans IQD 98.375–122.048 million, and the `6082-T6-proposed` 1,652.057 kg rollup spans IQD 9.268–11.498 million across 21 alternatives each. These are material + assumed labour + 20% sensitivity screens, not a compatible grade or product match, priced body, or complete domestic fabrication cost. The stock takeoff has no profile/sheet split, order quantity, purchase yield or actual shop hours; the Ur effective price date and commercial terms are unstated. Machining, joining, coating, tooling, equipment, QA, employer burden, utility, delivery, tax, installation and rework remain excluded. Concrete, rebar, aluminium and GFRP screens therefore have different source coverage and open cost scopes. The current [sourcing dossier](../design-engine/sourcing/README.md) records source dates, domestic manufacturer leads, packet identities and open costs.

The current procurement packet adds eight material-demand lines in kilograms or cubic metres. They retain all 2,269 source groups and 27,216 located occurrences, with exact source links and an explicit stock/member mass residual; the v10 packet also attaches the 42 aluminium cost sensitivities to the matching two material-demand lines. Seven lines carry domestic manufacturer discovery leads; rail-equivalent stock has no mapped domestic rail-profile lead in the current source set. All eight still have unknown commercial order quantities. Grade and product-form compatibility, yields, commercial units, standard fastener SKUs, complete bogie/brake assemblies, copper winding/testing, GFRP panels/tooling, and precast reinforcement, prestress, labour and transport remain open. The packages are not complete purchasing assemblies, and the 4,773 unlocated accounts remain outside their material quantities. These records still need to flow through one canonical candidate bundle to Iraqi installed rates, finite-resource factory/civil schedules, monthly cash requirements, ERP drafts, IFC properties and field records. The sensitivity-ranking implementation exists, but current BOM groups do not declare impact ranges, uncertainty bases, dependent decisions, alternatives or affected reruns, so it cannot yet prioritize the live backlog by numeric decision value. Missing prices or equipment remain unknown.

The v0.4 City Studio, Sim GUI, simulator and OCC GUI are restored as a source-pinned, isolated workspace at [`legacy/v0.4`](../../legacy/v0.4/README.md), including their component libraries and Samawah fixture. The current planning editor remains the v0.6 proposal handoff; the historical City Studio and simulation results do not feed the selected wheel/LIM candidate. ERPNext, Frappe HR, FUXA and the gateway remain deployable components with historical integration examples, but there is still no disposable current-candidate end-to-end workflow that provisions a v0.6 site, imports reviewed candidate records and exercises the owner/builder/operator lifecycle. That integration must preserve candidate identity and must not import maglev quantities or imply physical qualification. The full release-to-release comparison is recorded here so retained historical functionality is not mistaken for current v0.6 integration.

The 16-layer planning workflow provides a reviewed proposal handoff, but its historical route is not a substitute for site survey or operating evidence. The v0.4/v0.5 historical finance, city delivery and magnetic train results remain available at their pinned sources; current v0.6 city performance publication remains held until propulsion, braking, route, mass and cost evidence can support it.
