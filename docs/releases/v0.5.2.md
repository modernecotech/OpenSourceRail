# OpenSourceRail v0.5.2 development

This revision adopts station-powered ferrite maglev as the primary development
strategy. It is development on `main`; the published v0.5.1 release and tag remain
preserved. This document does not announce a new GitHub release or manufacturing
approval.

Station LSMs provide launch and receiving braking. Interstation propulsion is
unpowered; onboard PM-biased active support/guidance and a 30 kWh services battery
remain independent. The Baghdad study uses six 17 m bodies and four passenger
doors per car. Its central 14.2 t/car tare includes a 5.2 t equipped GFRP body,
3 t ferrite and the full 1.8 t growth reserve. These are unqualified mass budgets.

The revision adds reproducible jerk-limited trajectories, magnetic-band overlap,
station power/braking/charging and separate electrical cost sensitivities. The
nominal reference is 90 s over a flat 1 km span with 190 s headway. A 45 s charge
within 60 s dwell requires 1.20 MW/train before charger losses under the declared
hot-services assumption. Grade/coast/arrival margins and receiving readiness
remain route acceptance gates.

Quantity-derived GFRP panel cases, point-dipole reference benchmarks and linear
block-orientation inventories support design exploration. Ring-field gains earn
no railway lift or magnet-mass credit. The balanced civil candidate uses a 25 m,
0.80 × 1.80 m box with 100/150 mm web/flange thicknesses and end infills. Hollow
support and ground-specific foundation families replace universal support-size
assumptions in the study; no ground or structural acceptance is supplied.

The incomplete ordinary guideway cost comparison retains passive iron and shows
support/process targets separately from geometry savings. Station electrical
plant is additional scope. A two-shift bottleneck model tracks moulds, foundations,
piers, transport, cranes and final rail survey, with unexecuted strength and QA
hold points. Planning prices and output rates are not quotations or measured
production.

Persistent launch reservations and clearance checks support offline protocol
verification. A committed train keeps its corridor ownership after permission
expiry. Receiver failure, independent stopping/holding and stationary recovery
remain explicit design obligations. Physical actuator adapters, authenticated
sensors and hardware timing are not qualified.

The source-bound station-powered, station-structure, station-native and
station-protocol packets join the existing publication workflow. Images and
short assembly videos show partial geometry and final-position visibility;
they do not demonstrate motion, collision-free erection or deployment. The
coupled plant remains a stationary single car with ideal force actuators and a
flexible vertical beam, without a moving six-car system demonstration.

All design-completion, physical-validation, manufacturing-release and
physical-release flags remain false. Executed test/source receipts identify the
actual checked scope; no accepted physical engineering release is claimed.
