# Business, maintenance and renewal

A railway lasts through many generations of staff and equipment. A component
must be received, inspected, installed, maintained, replaced and eventually
retired. The information that follows it can make those tasks safer and less
expensive. OpenSourceRail connects engineering records with proposed
manufacturing, procurement, quality and maintenance workflows so that a
physical revision is visible beyond the design office.

## One component, one history

Consider a door operator. Its drawing defines its fit; its bill of materials
names the parts; a purchasing record identifies the supplier; incoming QA
checks the received item; assembly records where it was installed; maintenance
records faults, repair and replacement. A technician should be able to see
which revision is on the train and which inspection limits apply. If a
supplier changes the motor or a body mount moves, affected work instructions
and spare parts must change with it.

The current software can prepare candidate-linked drafts and has exercised
a bounded synthetic custody example. It can reject a purchased child without
a serial, batch, incoming inspection or valid parent consumption record. The
complete counted door module has not yet passed a real native ERPNext
receipt-to-assembly-to-replacement journey. That is an integration milestone,
not a detail to infer from the smaller example.

ERPNext and Frappe HR are relevant to purchasing, stores, work orders,
quality, people and training. The project gateway and FUXA can support
equipment observations and alarm history. These tools require a real owner,
permissions, warehouse and asset masters, authenticated devices and
commissioned procedures. A software demonstration does not create legal
purchasing authority, accepted equipment or a railway operating organization.

## Design for replacement

Maintenance planning begins before a part fails. The door needs isolation
points, access, lifting or handling provisions, a removal path, spare stock
and a post-replacement check. A service battery needs thermal management,
fire isolation and a cassette or other removal route. A guideway bearing
needs inspection and an eventual jacking and replacement method. These
choices alter mass, civil space, workshop tools and lifecycle cost.

The same principle applies to renewal of software and data. A city owner
should retain readable design information, open interfaces, licenses and
the ability to move to another supplier. Historical calculation records
should remain retrievable so a later engineer can understand why a design
was selected or rejected. Current work should be kept compact enough that
the evidence remains usable rather than overwhelming the ordinary checkout.

## People carry the system

Production and maintenance capability rests on trained people as much as
machines. Inspectors need approved criteria; technicians need safe work
instructions; planners need to know the assumptions behind costs; operators
need fault and rescue procedures. The project can help organize those
records, but a real railway would need an accountable employer, accepted
competence, independent checks and an operational safety system. The aim of
the open platform is to make that work visible and transferable, not to
replace it with a database entry.
