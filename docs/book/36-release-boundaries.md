# What v0.6.1 does not claim

The most useful software release is one whose limits are easy to
understand. OpenSourceRail v0.6.1 publishes a planning, research and
review platform. The release can run a Samawah study, display a city
atlas, expose source-linked engineering examples and prepare cost and
delivery drafts. It does not say that a vehicle, structure, station or
railway has passed physical acceptance.

## The map is not a survey

The 266-city catalogue is broad, but many routes, demands, rates and
site facts are provisional. A corridor may cross an unseen utility or
need different access from a map marker. A cost account may use a
transferred rate instead of a local quotation. The atlas helps choose
what to survey and compare; it cannot establish land rights, ground
conditions, accessibility or local demand without local evidence.

The Samawah fresh study is a software demonstration of a bounded city
calculation. The Baghdad packet is a retained, read-only study. They
are not two newly engineered city projects. The published atlas also
holds native RustGIS export because the GIS and outline inputs do not
match. A user should not infer from an editable map concept that a
complete native return and repricing journey shipped in v0.6.1.

## A model is not an installed vehicle

The wheel-supported six-car reference defines passenger places and
running gear for development. Its propulsion architecture, powered
length, braking envelope and protected service still need candidate-
specific engineering. Mass subtotals do not cover every installed
joint, cable, cooling unit, protection item or supplier detail.
Door, body, bogie and roof concepts need mating interfaces, fatigue,
fire, repair and physical testing. A rotatable 3D view or matching
CAD volume is valuable, but it does not certify the full assembly.

Likewise, a finite-element solver can finish a structural model and
still leave the real rail seat, bearings, foundations, construction
sequence or material behaviour outside that model. A propulsion
calculation can close energy numerically without demonstrating a
manufactured motor's force, current or repeated heat duty. Physical
limits require appropriate measurements and independent review.

## A draft is not an order or operating permission

The cost model's low, base and high cases are estimates under stated
assumptions. Its delivery tasks are planning drafts, not a release to
manufacture or construct. Business adapters can help prepare reviewed
records but do not authorize live purchasing. FUXA and gateway examples
can organize equipment observations but do not command a passenger
train. Rust control simulations have no certified movement authority.

An older integrated engineering dossier remains visibly held at this
release because its source binding is stale. The Workbench leaves global
engineering cost and candidate portfolio views unavailable rather than
showing a plausible but unsupported result. Historical archives remain
useful for research at their own source conditions; they cannot fill
the current dossier gap by being nearby in the repository.

## How a reader can use the release well

Use the city atlas to frame transport questions. Use the Samawah journey
to see how a changed demand produces a distinct result. Use the
Baghdad study to inspect a retained network comparison. Use the
engineering views to find fit, load and manufacturing questions. Use
the cost scopes to identify missing local prices, and the delivery
drafts to discuss factory and site capacity. At each step, ask what
would have to be measured or reviewed before the conclusion could
support a decision.

That approach preserves the release's real value. It gives cities and
contributors an open, inspectable starting point and a common language
for improving the design. The v0.7 programme described later in the
book extends the connected workflow, but it does not retroactively
change what v0.6.1 shipped or grant physical approval to its studies.

## What would strengthen the claims

A city planning claim becomes stronger with observed demand, accessible
walking surveys, verified alignment and local ground and utility
information. A cost claim becomes stronger with measured quantities,
supplier quotations, documented productivity and risk. A vehicle claim
needs installed dimensions and mass, verified joints and equipment,
physical motion and thermal tests, and independent review. A service
claim needs a validated available-force map, braking and rescue envelope,
protected control and operating procedures.

Those steps do not erase the value of the software release. They show
what work the release is meant to organize. A reader can identify the
next evidence item, connect it to the same candidate, and see which
conclusions change. That is the path from a public research platform
toward a railway a city could ultimately approve and support.
