# The v0.7 continuous project

The published v0.6.1 platform lets a reader explore city outlines and review
several useful engineering and delivery records. The v0.7 development
programme asks for a stronger connection: one change to a city corridor
should follow through the service, the selected train, civil work,
manufacture, cost and the dossier that explains the result. A reader should
be able to tell which parts changed, which remained valid and which are
still waiting for engineering evidence.

Imagine a planner moving a station entrance. The location could change the
walk that passengers take, a train's dwell time, energy use, the quantities
of access structure and the construction allowance. It should not cause an
unchanged vehicle door to be analysed again. If a vehicle revision changes
the door, on the other hand, its mass, structure, wiring, manufacture and
maintenance consequences should travel together. This is the practical
meaning of project continuity.

## A project has one accountable design

The proposed v0.7 journey keeps a corridor revision, vehicle candidate,
civil option, service plan and cost basis attached to each result. The
physical vehicle and the question being asked of it are distinct. A door
may have the same geometry while receiving a new test under a different
load. A research propulsion alternative may require a different bogie,
electrical equipment and mass, and therefore becomes a different physical
candidate. A selection is yet another decision: why one of the alternatives
is being carried forward for a stated purpose.

These distinctions protect a reader from a tempting but false composite.
A fleet count from one corridor cannot automatically price another.
Structural results for one body cannot validate a modified body. A
powered-wheel research car cannot borrow the passive reference's nominal
mass or braking result. The Workbench is intended to make such boundaries
visible without asking every reader to open a numerical archive.

## What already works

The first v0.7 comparison is available in the development Workbench. It
recalculates the published Samawah network and a proposed merger of two
nearby station markers. The reader can open before-and-after dossiers,
inspect all fourteen cost scopes, see a revised delivery draft and download
a comparison. The edit keeps the vehicle and unchanged civil quantities
attached to their existing evidence. Mixed candidates and stale upstream
stages are refused rather than quietly combined.

The city calculation can queue bounded studies and reuse a verified result
for an identical request. That speeds up review and reduces needless work,
but it is only valid when the relevant inputs and methods are identical.
The platform records both fresh calculations and reused work so a warm
result is not described as a newly executed one.

## What continuity still needs

The corridor comparison does not yet make a complete installed railway.
The exact six-car train, its mated doors and equipment, selected drive,
brakes, civil support, construction method and candidate-linked production
documents still need to be brought into one reviewable project. The current
door panel exposes useful counted parts and open fit questions; it is not a
released door assembly. The GIS return path has completed automated
native-tool round trips for Samawah and Baghdad, but a full supported browser journey
through the native editor still needs its own demonstration.

The aim is not to make every box green by assumption. A complete project
can be useful while showing that a platform location is unsurveyed, a
motor force map is unavailable or an inspection limit has not been
approved. It tells the city what decision can be made now and what evidence
must come next. That is a more useful meaning of progress than a large
collection of disconnected successful calculations.

## A useful change report

For a city reader, the most helpful report begins with the parent
proposal, the new proposal and a short explanation of the edit. It then
separates direct changes from consequences. A moved stop directly changes
its location; changed line time or access cost is a calculated consequence.
It also explains why apparently related values, such as the fleet or a
vehicle part count, stayed fixed. This makes a small edit an opportunity
to understand the railway rather than just compare two large totals.

For an engineer, the same report points to the exact load, mass, geometry
and material assumptions behind each result. For a workshop, it indicates
whether a drawing or part number has changed. For an owner, it shows which
prices are local observations and which are transferred estimates. These
are different views of the same candidate, not separate versions of the
truth. If one view depends on information that has not been supplied,
the report should say so and leave that conclusion open.
