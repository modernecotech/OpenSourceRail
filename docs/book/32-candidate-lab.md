# Exploring candidate research

The platform is designed to compare railway candidates, not only to display
one preferred train. The published development reference is a passive
wheel-supported six-car formation. It leaves installed propulsion open and
retains a passive interface for linear-motor research. Earlier and separate
candidates explore powered wheels, station drives, recovery equipment and
different structures. A useful comparison asks how each candidate delivers
the same passenger service with its own complete installed system.

## Keep the three identities apart

A **physical candidate** describes what the railway is: train, running
gear, propulsion interfaces, civil form and relevant installation details.
An **evaluation** describes a calculation or test of that candidate under
declared loads, assumptions and tool conditions. A **selection** is a
decision to carry one candidate forward for a stated purpose. A change in
motor model might create a new evaluation of the same geometry. A new
bogie, body or station layout changes the physical candidate itself.

This matters when a research concept looks promising. A candidate with a
low preliminary beam mass cannot inherit another vehicle's braking, cost
or fleet result. A different propulsion distribution may move weight from
the train to stations, change electrical duty and change maintenance and
recovery work. Comparing only the attractive component would miss the
whole railway.

## What the published release makes available

The v0.6.1 Workbench shows the selected passive-wheel development reference.
Its current interactive portfolio selector is held because the integrated
engineering dossier no longer matches its recorded source. A reader should
not expect a live drop-down of accepted current candidates there. The
source-pinned coupled-railway archive remains available for **read-only
research comparison** at its historical source conditions. It includes a
station linear-motor case and a separately identified powered-wheel case.

The powered-wheel record is a definition study. It lacks an available-force
map and has unresolved installed mass and price for the additional equipment.
It does not prove that powered wheels can meet the timetable or that they
cost less. The station-drive case also has model and hardware limits. The
archive is valuable for seeing how the project frames alternatives and
preserves their ancestry; it does not elevate either concept to a qualified
installation.

## Read a comparison without choosing too early

Begin by stating the passenger service: route, demand, places, journey time,
availability and accessibility. Then compare each candidate's vehicle
mass, station equipment, energy, braking, civil loads, manufacturing route,
repair access and whole-life cost. Where an account is unknown, mark it
unknown or give a justified bound. Do not treat the missing motor, cooling
or protection package as a zero-cost benefit.

The project also contains architecture searches, adaptive design and
generative structural experiments. Its outline portfolio names 112 railway
bundles, 54 body studies and 18 civil spans. These figures describe the
breadth of digital exploration, not the number of physically accepted
trains. A screen can identify a promising family or a physical contradiction.
It cannot substitute for detailed installation, manufacturing and testing.

A good research result may be a failure with a clear reason. An axial
mechanism can fail a pin-jointed load path without disproving a bonded shell
that carries force differently. A motor calculation can close numerical
energy without representing the complete inverter and thermal duty. The
record should say what was tested, what failed and what next experiment
would change the decision. This makes the archive useful to future teams
without turning old calculations into current approval.

## Decide what experiment comes next

When two concepts have overlapping cost ranges, another complete campaign
may add little. The better next experiment may be a supplier mass estimate,
a thermal test of a short drive section, a full-size door removal trial or
a surveyed foundation. Its value depends on whether it could change the
choice. Conversely, if a candidate violates a genuine load or clearance
requirement, the team should revise its geometry before paying for a
fine mesh. This staged approach preserves resources for the promising
families without discarding an unfamiliar idea because a simplified model
cannot represent its real load path.

The research archive also has a civic value. A local manufacturer can see
which components might be made or repaired nearby and which need
qualification or foreign sourcing. A city can see whether a proposed
saving in train hardware shifts cost into stations or construction. An
independent reviewer can reproduce a bounded calculation and identify
where physical evidence begins. Open design knowledge is most useful
when it keeps alternatives and failed attempts as carefully as the
current favourite.
