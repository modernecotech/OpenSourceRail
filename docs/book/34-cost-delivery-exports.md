# Reading costs and delivery drafts

The platform tries to price a railway from the work it would require,
not from a single allowance per kilometre. A city estimate connects
route length and station pattern to trains, civil quantities, power,
staff, production, maintenance and renewal. This is why the published
Samawah journey has many outputs. Each answers a different part of the
question, “What would it take to provide this service?”

## Fourteen scopes, one candidate

The cost model separates rolling stock, guideway, stations and power,
factory and tooling, construction, land, utilities and depot work, fleet,
energy, operations, maintenance, renewals, recovery, reliability and
decommissioning. These scopes help prevent a low vehicle purchase price
from hiding a large station or maintenance cost. They also make a route
edit easier to understand: a changed station may affect access, energy
or operating work even if guideway length does not change.

Low, base and high figures are scenarios under declared quantities, rates
and uncertainty. They are not a probability distribution by themselves,
and they are not three competing contractor bids. A rate may come from
a dated observation, an engineering calculation or a transferred
assumption from another place. A reader should look for that distinction
before comparing totals. Supplier quotations, local wages, actual ground
and utility conditions and finance terms can all move the ranking.

There are also different cost boundaries. A part's factory cost covers
stock and proposed process. Delivery adds transport and handling.
Installation adds site labour, fixtures, wiring, testing and temporary
works. Whole-life cost follows energy, staffing, maintenance, renewal,
reliability and eventual removal. An early material subtotal is never a
complete train price. A city capital estimate is never automatically a
whole-life judgement.

## A delivery draft is a proposal for work

The Samawah result can be accompanied by a delivery plan. It takes
modelled quantities and produces possible work and resource tasks. This
helps a team ask whether factory capacity, shifts, precast handling,
construction access and inspection have been considered. It does not
establish an actual crew calendar, signed purchase order or approved
construction method. An estimate of process hours, for example, needs
measured shop performance and the selected equipment before it can
become a committed production schedule.

The optional business draft is deliberately reviewable and disabled.
Its purpose is to show how candidate-linked engineering records might
enter a project and purchasing system after people agree on the parts,
suppliers, inspection and authority to transact. Turning a calculated
design into a live order requires a separate human and organizational
decision. The same boundary applies to any draft maintenance or workforce
record.

## Use exports to ask a decision question

The short summary makes the case legible to a stakeholder. The detailed
result allows an engineer or planner to inspect service and scope. The
source record identifies the chosen input context. The performance record
explains whether the result was freshly calculated or safely reused. The
delivery plan gives production and construction questions a first shape.
Together they make it possible for two people to discuss the same case
without passing around an unexplained screenshot.

Suppose a city considers a shorter station spacing. It should inspect
passenger access and journey time, then fleet and energy, then station and
civil quantities, then construction disruption and whole-life cost. If
the result changes only a small capital account but changes operations
over decades, that difference deserves attention. If a site cost is
unknown, the model should keep the uncertainty visible and identify
the survey or quotation that would most improve the choice.

The release's cost and delivery outputs are useful design-stage tools.
They become stronger as local evidence replaces generic assumptions.
They do not approve a budget, contract, manufacturing route or railway
operation on their own.

## Compare equal offers of service

A low-cost option may appear inexpensive because it has fewer trains,
shorter operating hours or a longer passenger wait. A fair comparison
states how many people must be carried, what access and journey quality
they receive, and how reliably the railway must run. Each candidate can
meet those needs differently, but its station, fleet, power, workshop and
renewal consequences must remain in the same estimate. Mandatory safety
and accessibility costs cannot be excluded to make a design look
affordable.

If low, base and high ranges overlap, the result may be “more evidence
needed” rather than a winner. The platform can then direct attention to
the rate or physical quantity with the strongest influence. This is a
productive outcome: a targeted ground survey or supplier quotation may
resolve more uncertainty than a broad round of numerical refinement.
