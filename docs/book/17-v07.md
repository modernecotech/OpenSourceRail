# One complete candidate for v0.7

The immediate v0.7 target is a journey through one project. A user changes a
Samawah station or alignment and sees the resulting service, fleet, civil,
energy, cost and dossier changes. From that same project, they can open one
vehicle module and follow its geometry, manufacture, inspection and
maintenance. Every result should refer to the same candidate or explain why
it is a separately evaluated alternative.

## The corridor and its train

The Samawah midpoint comparison is the first working step. The next work
should bind the corridor to one selected six-car installed candidate, one
representative civil and station arrangement, and a propulsion/braking
evaluation that can support the proposed journey. A station edit should
recalculate only its dependants. A vehicle change should trigger the mass,
force, structure, manufacturing and cost work it actually affects. The
Workbench should show those changes and the open site inputs without asking
a reader to inspect a directory of numerical archives.

Baghdad provides the second city test. Repeating the same method on a
different corridor checks that the project is not tuned only to one set of
Samawah assumptions. It also exposes which rates, route conventions and
station interfaces are genuinely shared and which must be replaced with
local information.

## One module through its life

The door operator is the representative module. Its inventory already
reveals much of the required work; v0.7 needs a mated, installed design with
controlled header, rail, carriage, drive and sensor dimensions. Door motion
and removal must fit the vehicle. The same module should then have a
manufacturing drawing set, tolerances, tools, assembly operations, incoming
inspection and service replacement instructions. Its actual candidate-linked
bill of materials can be carried through business and maintenance records.

This is a deliberately narrow demonstration. A full train has many other
critical systems, and physical passenger safety still requires more than a
working software journey. One complete, reviewable path through the tools is
more valuable than thousands of superficially filled records.

## Engineering work that follows

The vehicle team must close installed mass, equipment access, joints and
running gear. The propulsion team must establish available force, electrical
and thermal duty, and independent stopping. The civil team must model the
actual rail and support interface, bearings, foundation and erection. Cost
and service work must compare alternatives at equal passenger service with
the same candidate quantities. Physical tests, supplier information and
independent review have their own milestones.

The platform's tool choices should follow that work. A measured slow stage
may justify more Rust or vectorisation, while a dependency error should be
fixed before making the wrong calculation faster. SolidCraft, FieldCraft and
RustGIS can become preferred where they have demonstrated the required
geometry, mechanics or editing domain. Other tools remain valuable for
independent checks until an equivalent route has been shown. Historical
research stays retrievable even when an obsolete default installation is
retired.

The outcome sought for v0.7 is a comprehensible comparison and a genuine
production handoff, with its unresolved evidence plainly stated. That is the
right foundation for a physical prototype and, later, a railway that a city
can understand, build and maintain.
