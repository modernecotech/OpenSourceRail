# Networks, service and city variety

OpenSourceRail contains planning outlines for 266 cities. This breadth is
useful for exploring how different urban patterns might influence a railway.
It is not a claim that 266 independent surveyed, engineered and priced systems
are ready to build. Many city outlines use shared assumptions or transferred
rates. A city dossier can help an owner ask better questions while remaining
clear about the evidence that is still local and absent.

## One city, several possible services

An alignment is only part of a network. The number and position of stations,
interchanges, service hours, frequency, train formation and spare fleet affect
the usefulness and cost of the railway. A dense centre may reward shorter walks
and frequent stops; a longer peripheral corridor may need a different spacing
or an express pattern. The same train may not be the lowest-cost choice for
both. Passenger demand by time of day matters as much as a total city
population.

The software can model station counts, route lengths, timetables, fleet and
energy under stated assumptions. It can compare low, base and high cost cases
across capital, operating, renewal and financing accounts. These are design
stage scenarios. They cannot replace observed demand, protected headway,
supplier prices, actual ground conditions or approved operations. Where a
route is drawn through uncertain terrain, the corresponding civil and risk
cost should remain provisional.

## Geography and economics must move together

A change in route may alter length, curves, gradients, bridges, station access
and land take. That can change travel time and energy as well as structures and
cost. A change in demand can alter the fleet and power requirement even when
the line stays in place. Conversely, a change in local wages should reprice the
applicable labour work without pretending the railway geometry changed. This
is why the planning platform keeps a relationship between inputs and results,
instead of publishing a new opaque total after every edit.

Baghdad is a useful second test after Samawah because its corridor and city
context differ. A native GIS workflow has returned a sequence of station edits
from both cities to the calculation and back to a refreshed project. That
checks the continuity of the software route. The engineering case still needs
its own surveyed alignment, site-specific structures, station access, demand
and candidate-specific operating assumptions.

## From outline to city decision

The first decision might be which corridor deserves a survey, where an
interchange should be studied, or which train and civil family merit a detailed
comparison. It need not be a commitment to construct. A strong city proposal
states its transport objective and alternatives, records the inputs, shows
the cost and service sensitivities, and lists the missing evidence likely to
change the ranking. It also includes the people who will use the system:
wheelchair users, older passengers, children, shift workers and those whose
journey crosses the proposed line rather than following it.

The 266-city catalogue is a broad planning library. The detailed v0.7 work
concentrates on one Samawah corridor, then one Baghdad corridor, so that a
complete change can be followed through design, cost and delivery before the
same method is offered more widely.
