# Current results and open questions

The v0.6.1 Open Engineering Platform is a published software release. It
offers planning and research tools, a Workbench, candidate-linked examples
and documented limitations. The Samawah software journey has been installed
and demonstrated from the published release. That supports the claim that
the software route can be used and reviewed. It does not make a railway
physically accepted or a city design ready for construction.

Development since that release has joined the Samawah station edit to
service, fleet, fourteen cost scopes and a revised dossier view. Native GIS
edits have returned through Samawah and Baghdad calculations. Candidate
studies have counted vehicle parts and door components, explored new CAD and
mechanics backends, and compared independent numerical results. These are
meaningful steps toward a complete engineering workflow. Their findings
still belong to the exact candidates and model domains used to produce them.

| Area | What exists | What still decides the physical design |
|---|---|---|
| Corridor | Comparative route, station, service and cost calculations | Surveyed access, demand, land, ground, utilities and chosen service |
| Vehicle | Counted components, candidate geometry and analytical controls | Installed fit, complete mass, joints, dynamics, fire and passenger tests |
| Propulsion and braking | Conditional force, energy and stopping studies | Available installed force, repeated thermal duty and validated braking |
| Civil works | Span and moving-load models with independent comparisons | Bearings, rail seats, foundations, fatigue and erection at the actual site |
| Manufacture | Proposed routes, stock, BOM and assembly order | Controlled drawings, qualified processes, supplier evidence and QA limits |
| Lifecycle | Draft business and maintenance handoffs | Actual candidate-linked transactions, organizations and reviewed records |

## A numerical result has a boundary

Two solvers can agree closely on a model while its real bearing, fastening or
material behaviour remains uncertain. A finite-element mesh can refine toward
a stable displacement without proving that the chosen load case is complete.
A motor method can reproduce an ideal field while leaving the manufactured
primary, current closure and heat transfer absent. The correct response is
to keep the useful numerical evidence and name the next measurement or model
needed to make a physical decision.

The current installed vehicle still needs resolved door and equipment
interfaces, removal paths, mass and inertia. Propulsion needs force-speed and
repeated-duty maps linked to the chosen train and corridor. Braking needs
independent stopping envelopes. Civil work needs a represented rail/support
interface, foundations, moving and accidental loads, and a complete erection
method. Cost needs equal-service comparisons, site quantities and stronger
supplier evidence. These questions are connected: a heavier fitted vehicle
changes the beam and braking problem, while a changed station changes service
and energy.

The older integrated reference dossier is retained with a source-binding
hold. Its attractive global cards are not treated as current supported
results. Historical maglev and other research remain available to study;
they do not replace the wheel-supported reference by proximity in a folder
or a book. This separation protects both the history and the present design.
