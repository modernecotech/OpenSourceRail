# Who can use the platform now

There are several useful starting points, depending on what a reader knows
and what decision they need to make. None requires pretending that a railway
is already approved. The most productive first use is a bounded question:
Which corridor deserves survey? Which service assumption drives fleet size?
What part of a proposed train still lacks an installed interface? Where
would a workshop need a drawing or a qualified supplier before it could
make a module?

## A city or public owner

A city team can start with the mapped catalogue and the planning workspace.
It can inspect a proposed line, stop locations and nearby context, then add
local knowledge about walking access, demand, land, utilities or flooding.
The planning handoff records route, stop, service and origin–destination
proposals. That handoff is a proposal, not a costed or validated railway.
A calculated city study is a different object: it also carries modelled
service, fleet, energy, quantities and costs under stated assumptions.

This distinction helps an owner commission the right next work. A new stop
may demand a catchment survey before its price is worth refining. A new
headway may require a protected-service and fleet study. A guideway crossing
may require ground and utility evidence before choosing a span. Samawah
shows how even a small marker edit can reveal these dependencies. Baghdad
provides a different large-city planning context, with a retained
read-only comparison in v0.6.1. Neither example is a substitute for local
transport engagement or engineering surveys.

## A builder or local workshop

A fabrication team can use the vehicle and civil studies to ask whether a
proposed part suits its available materials, machines, quality methods and
transport. The assembly views, parts catalogue and make-or-buy records are
designed to expose the number of items and operations hidden by a concept
picture. They can support requests for information and a prototype plan.
Before making a safety-relevant part, the team still needs controlled
dimensions and tolerances, process qualifications, inspection limits and
responsibility for changes.

The door operator is a useful example. Its counted inventory is detailed,
yet several mating positions and the full removal route remain unresolved.
A workshop contribution that establishes those physical interfaces, or
shows why a proposed guide cannot be removed, may be more valuable than a
larger set of attractive renderings. The same principle applies to precast
spans: the ability to cast a beam is only one part of transporting, lifting,
connecting and inspecting it.

## A university, reviewer or engineer

Researchers can reproduce a declared calculation, compare candidate
families, challenge an assumption and identify a useful measurement. The
project retains failed and historical work because it can show where an
idea or a model stopped being credible. A close match between two solvers
is useful evidence for the exact model they share. It does not prove that
an unmodelled bearing or joint is safe. A strong review states which claim
has been tested, which evidence supports it and what remains uncertain.

The software's source-bound records make disagreements more productive.
Instead of arguing over a single cost or displacement value, collaborators
can identify the candidate, service, load, rate or material assumption that
produced it. They can then propose a new evaluation without overwriting
the earlier result. Physical measurements and independent checks should be
added as their own evidence, rather than retroactively changing what an
old simulation meant.

## A future deployment partner

An organization considering the business and supervision components can
test them in a disposable environment using its own roles, item masters,
warehouses, inspection steps and equipment model. Draft records should be
reviewed before any real purchase, work order or asset installation. A
gateway should be bound to known devices and kept outside movement
authority unless an independently approved control system permits it.

The path from demonstration to operation is a sequence of local decisions:
select a bounded project, name its owners, establish site and supplier
evidence, develop the installed candidate, review production and safety
records, build and test, and commission under the applicable authority.
The platform can organize those decisions. The organizations responsible
for a real railway must make and approve them.
