# Capacity, frequency and useful service

A railway can carry many people on paper and still offer an awkward journey.
The passenger does not experience a capacity figure; they experience the walk
to the station, the wait, the ability to board, the time on board and the
certainty of reaching their destination. Capacity has to be tested against
those experiences, and against what the railway can deliver reliably on an
ordinary hot afternoon, not only under a perfect timetable.

## Places and trains are different measures

The six-car reference has 44 fixed seats and two wheelchair places in each
car. Across the formation that is 264 fixed seats and twelve wheelchair
places, or 276 named passenger places. At one train every two minutes, simple
arithmetic gives 30 trains per hour and 8,280 places per hour per direction.
This is a useful reference control for comparing concepts. It is not an
operating capacity until station dwell, safe train separation, terminal
turnback, boarding patterns, availability and passenger demand have been
shown to support it together.

The project retains a 120-person-per-train comparison that yields 14,400
places per hour at the same two-minute interval. That is an alternative
formation and accommodation question, not a property of the seated reference
train. The 160-person-per-car crowd load is a structural control. It asks
whether the body can withstand a demanding occupancy case; it does not tell
the operator to plan standing passengers in everyday service. Keeping these
three quantities distinct prevents a strong beam calculation from becoming
an unintended passenger policy.

## The bottleneck moves

A shorter headway can increase offered places, but it also makes dwell
variation more important. A wheelchair boarding event, a busy interchange
or a door fault can hold the following train unless the stations, control
system and recovery plan have enough margin. More trains require more
vehicles, more power, more inspections and often more depot capacity. A
longer train may relieve crowding but lengthens platforms, increases station
and structure loads, and can complicate access on a tight site.

The retained-stop reference uses a 72 km/h launch cap. It is a design
control for the journey calculation, not a promise that every alignment can
reach that speed. Acceleration, braking, gradients and closely spaced stops
can dominate travel time. So can a station placed on the wrong side of a
road. A useful service comparison therefore holds the actual corridor and
passenger objective constant while varying train length, frequency or
propulsion. Otherwise a cheap vehicle can appear attractive only because
it offers fewer journeys or serves fewer people.

| Decision | Passenger effect | Engineering consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Add a station | Shorter access for some riders; longer ride for others | Platform, civil work, dwell, power and fleet may change |
| Run more often | Shorter average wait and more offered places | More trains, energy, staff and protected control capacity |
| Use a longer train | More places per departure | Longer platforms and different structural loads |
| Change stopping pattern | Different travel and transfer times | New timetable, capacity and recovery analysis |

## Reliability is part of capacity

An offered timetable is only useful when the fleet and infrastructure can
maintain it. The model should include spare trains, inspection time,
maintenance access, station equipment faults, power outages and the time
needed to rescue a stopped train. A failure that blocks a single guideway
can turn a high theoretical capacity into a long queue. The cost of that
queue matters to a city's decision even if it does not appear on the
construction bill.

OpenSourceRail's direction is to compare complete service packages. A
candidate needs a route, station pattern, train and propulsion arrangement,
protected separation concept, maintenance plan and cost range. The current
tools can connect many of these assumptions and expose what changes after
an edit. The passenger demand, safety margins and failure rates needed for
an operating timetable still require local evidence and independent review.
