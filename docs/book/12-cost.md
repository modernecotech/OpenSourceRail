# Cost and whole-life decisions

Affordability is a property of the complete service, not a single vehicle or
span price. A city needs to know what it would spend to design, build, equip,
operate, maintain, renew and eventually remove the railway. It also needs to
know what passenger capacity and journey quality those costs buy. A fair
comparison holds the service objective and its safety obligations constant
while allowing competing designs to meet them differently.

## Build a price from physical work

A useful estimate follows quantities and production routes. A beam needs
concrete, reinforcement or tendons, forms, labour, curing space, lifting,
transport, bearings, erection and rail fixing. A carbody panel needs stock,
cutting, forming, joining, finishing, fasteners, inspection and replacement
planning. Bought specialist equipment needs a specification, delivery,
integration, testing and eventual renewal. A unit price with no scope
definition can hide any of these steps.

The platform keeps factory, delivered, installed and whole-life costs
separate. It compares low, base and high design-stage scenarios with explicit
rates and allowances. Fourteen cost scopes connect different parts of the
railway, including rolling stock, guideway, stations, propulsion and power,
construction, operations, maintenance and finance. A rate borrowed from
another city is a provisional assumption. A supplier quotation can strengthen
one account without making all the other accounts certain.

| Cost boundary | Examples of questions |
|---|---|
| Factory | How much stock, labour, machine time, tooling and QA does the part need? |
| Delivered | What are packaging, transport, duties, handling and supplier terms? |
| Installed | What civil works, wiring, lifts, testing and site access are needed? |
| Whole life | How often are parts renewed, what energy and staff are needed, and what remains at disposal? |

The vehicle's detailed parts proposal has a base direct parts-and-process
subtotal of about $1.064 million across six cars. It excludes final assembly,
shared plant, delivery, qualification, warranty and many whole-life accounts.
It is not the purchase price of a finished train. Likewise, the Samawah
comparison's base installed capital is about $1.14 billion and its whole-life
present cost about $2.26 billion under its own planning assumptions. Neither
figure is a construction bid or a city budget.

## Price service, not just hardware

Fleet size follows route time, frequency, availability and spare policy. A
station change can alter energy or access even if it leaves the number of
operational stops and trains unchanged. A different car formation can alter
platform length, depot space and civil loads. A powered-wheel train might
reduce station equipment but increase vehicle mass and maintenance. These
cross-effects are why each economic result must retain its physical and
service candidate.

Current scenarios use declared production and civil shift patterns and
separate operating-hour cases. They are planning assumptions, not an approved
roster or construction schedule. Wages, productivity, supplier prices and
ground conditions can all change a ranking. The model should show where a
change matters most, so the next survey or quotation is chosen for its
decision value.

Affordability is not achieved by erasing a mandatory safety item, calling an
unknown mass zero or postponing a required test outside the cost boundary.
A more honest early estimate may be wider, but it gives the owner a sounder
basis for reducing cost through design, production and service choices.
