# Keeping the work open and useful

Open design knowledge is valuable when another team can understand what was
assumed, what was tested and what changed. A drawing with no dimensions is
hard to manufacture. A cost with no scope is hard to compare. A numerical
result with no candidate or load case is easy to misuse. OpenSourceRail
therefore treats explanation, evidence and revision history as parts of the
engineering product, not as paperwork added after the technical work.

## A contribution can be small and decisive

A useful contribution may correct a unit, document an overlooked doorway
clearance, test a control fault, check a supplier's bearing specification,
or improve a city's walking-access information. Practitioners in operations,
maintenance, quality, civil works and vehicle assembly can identify failures
that are difficult to see in a software model. Local knowledge can overturn
an apparently convenient station location or production assumption.

The project welcomes focused changes, reproducible observations and
independent reviews. A proposal should explain the condition under which it
applies and what it would change. If a new vehicle part changes mass or
clearance, its effects should be followed into braking, civil load,
manufacturing and cost. If a city edit changes only a labour rate, the
geometry need not be redrawn. This keeps open collaboration fast enough to
be useful while preserving the meaning of earlier work.

## Distinguish review from approval

An independent engineer can confirm that a calculation was reproduced or
challenge its model domain. A production reviewer can approve a drawing
for a stated prototype. A railway operator and its assessors must address
the larger deployment safety case. These are related but different acts.
The repository's software tests and research archives provide inputs to
review; they do not appoint an operator, qualify hardware or grant movement
authority.

Open work also means preserving negative results. A beam that failed a
load case, a motor calculation outside its tested speed, or a door removal
path that collides with the body should remain visible with the condition
that caused the finding. Later work may repair it or choose another family.
Deleting the failure would erase useful design knowledge and make a later
selection harder to trust.

## Licensing and stewardship

The project uses different open licences for different kinds of work:
Apache 2.0 for software, CERN-OHL-S v2 for open physical designs and
CC-BY-SA 4.0 for documentation, subject to the file-specific notices and
upstream rights recorded in the project. That split is intended to let
software, drawings and explanations be shared and improved while keeping
their origins clear. A contributor should check the terms of any supplier
drawing, data source, image or code before adding it.

The project is currently a pre-1.0, founder-led effort. Maintainers review
changes to the shared repository; domain reviewers examine the subjects
they know; a deployment partner supplies local site, equipment and
operational evidence. Larger changes to architecture, safety claims or
city economics deserve a written rationale and review. A promising open
design can become a reliable public asset only when the people who will
build, maintain and operate it can also understand and govern it.

The next chapters and releases should continue to serve two audiences at
once: the general reader who needs a clear account of the railway, and the
specialist who needs exact evidence. This ebook provides the first path.
Maintained engineering records and their separate verification receipts
provide the second. Both are necessary for a project that aims to remain
locally useful long after its first prototype.
