# M6 — Incident site access

**Scope:** response-crew access to derailment / fire / crash
sites. Coordination with emergency services and site
preservation.

**Cross-refs:** RFC 0013 §§4.4 M6 + §7 incident categorisation.

## Rules

### M6.1 — Site access authorisation

Access to an incident site is authorised only under a formal
M2 work block, or under an explicit emergency authorisation
issued by the OCC (name + timestamp + scope). Self-
dispatched recovery crews are turned away at the
protection signal.

**Why:** well-intentioned crews arriving unannounced are a
hazard to emergency services and contaminate the incident
scene. The authorisation channels all access through the
OCC, which keeps the site count controllable.

### M6.2 — Crash-site preservation

At a crash or derailment site, rolling stock is left as-
found for the NRSA investigator. Movement is authorised
only after the investigator releases the site, or in the
specific case where movement is necessary to free trapped
passengers (in which case a named OCC authorisation with
timestamp is recorded).

**Why:** moving evidence is how cause determination gets
closed as "undetermined". The trapped-passengers exception
is the only override; it is rare and must be documented.

### M6.3 — Fire site

At an onboard fire, civil fire services lead the response on
arrival. The maintenance crew's role is limited to: confirm
`osr-fire-safety` onboard systems have actuated (suppression
discharged, traction isolated), provide traction-battery
isolation information to the fire officer, and brief the LFP chemistry,
affected module/string,
outward vent direction, mist reservoir/pump/pressure/flow state, and the last
recorded module temperatures/off-gas readings. No person opens a battery
compartment, restores HV, or assumes the event is extinguished because visible
flame has stopped without fire-service authority and a documented thermal
monitoring period.

**Why:** fire services are trained for fire; rail crews are
trained for rail. The crew's job is to give the fire
officer the information they need to fight the fire safely
without pretending to lead a task the crew is not trained
for.

### M6.4 — Crane operations at site

On-site crane operations (recovering a derailed trainset)
require a qualified operator (per M1.3), an additional
lift plan reviewed by the M&E engineer, and spotters at
both ends of the lift. Multi-lift (two cranes on one load)
requires a specific multi-lift plan.

**Why:** on-site crane work is higher-risk than depot crane
work — uneven ground, time pressure, media attention. The
additional plan requirement is the compensating control.

### M6.5 — Evidence chain

Before any recovery move, the crew photographs the site
comprehensively (GPS-tagged, timestamped), extracts the
event recorder per D8, and makes a sealed copy for the
investigator. The chain-of-custody form is countersigned
by the crew lead and OCC duty officer.

**Why:** recovery destroys the spatial evidence;
photographs and the event recorder preserve the information
content of the site. The chain-of-custody form makes the
evidence admissible.

### M6.6 — Recovery move

A recovery move (derailed or crashed trainset being moved
off the line) operates at 15 km/h maximum, with physical
escort fore and aft, and carries no passengers or
personnel inside the trainset being recovered.

**Why:** recovery moves use compromised rolling stock on
possibly compromised track — the speed cap keeps kinetic
energy low. The no-passengers rule prevents a bad move
becoming a mass-casualty event.
