# M1 — Depot safety

**Scope:** hazard awareness, PPE, lone-worker protocol, pit work,
crane operations.

**Cross-refs:** RFC 0014 §5 (main-heavy layout), RFC 0013 §4.4 M1.

## Rules

### M1.1 — PPE baseline

All depot workers on the shop floor wear hi-vis vest, steel-
toe footwear, hearing protection in designated noise zones,
and eye protection when grinding, welding, or handling
compressed gas. Entry to the shop without baseline PPE is
refused by the gate.

**Why:** the baseline is enforced at the gate because mid-
shop "just popping in" is when PPE is forgotten and how
eye injuries happen. The gate is the single chokepoint that
catches it.

### M1.2 — Pit work

Pit work (under-car inspection or maintenance) requires: pit
lighting on, pit edge rails in place, no-unaccompanied rule
(minimum 2 workers, one at ground level), and the trainset
power-isolated and chocked.

**Why:** a worker falling into a pit or a trainset rolling
over a pit worker is catastrophic. The paired-work rule
means help is on site; the chock rule means the trainset
can't move.

### M1.3 — Crane operations

Overhead crane (40 t in main-heavy, 10 t in secondary-medium)
operations require a qualified operator (licensed per
deployment regulator), a clear area below the hook path (no
workers in the shadow), and a spotter in radio contact with
the operator.

**Why:** a lifted load swinging into a worker kills; the
spotter's job is specifically to see what the operator can't
from the cab. The qualification requirement is the legal
minimum and the insurance precondition.

### M1.4 — Lock-out tag-out (LOTO)

Before working on energised systems, the responsible
technician applies a personal LOTO tag to each energy source:
traction battery main isolator, auxiliary 400 VAC breaker,
compressed-air reservoir bleed valve. Each tag carries the
technician's name; only they can remove it.

**Why:** "someone else will check" is how people get
electrocuted. The named-tag-per-source rule is defence in
depth; tag removal by anyone other than the owner is a
disciplinary offence.

### M1.5 — Lone-worker

After-hours lone work (single technician on depot floor) is
permitted only with an active radio check-in every 30
minutes to the OCC night watch. A missed check-in triggers
a welfare call and, if unanswered, dispatch of a second
worker to investigate.

**Why:** a lone worker injured and unable to call for help
is invisible for the rest of the shift otherwise. The
30-minute cadence bounds the worst-case discovery delay.

### M1.6 — Chemical safety

Cleaning agents, lubricants, and gels are stored in a
designated chemical cabinet with labelled containers and
Safety Data Sheets (SDS) accessible at the cabinet. Mixing
chemicals or transferring to unlabelled containers is
prohibited.

**Why:** decanted-into-water-bottle solvent is the classic
depot poisoning; mixed bleach + ammonia is the classic
depot gassing. The cabinet and labels defeat both.
